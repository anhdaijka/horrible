# Discovery slate — <chapter>

Internal divergent work. Not writer context, not approved prose, and not authority merely because an option appears here.

## Fixed truth and open space

Compactly separate the approved purpose/outcome/reveal locks from execution space. Do not begin by outlining beats.

## Horror and mystery promise

What specific ghostly, dreadful or spiritually uncanny experience can this chapter give? What question or contradiction can the reader carry? How may the reader gain an evidentiary edge without turning a protagonist into the sole detective?

## Life already moving

What work, family business, errand, affection, embarrassment, money problem, social obligation, private concern or material process would exist if the main plot did not arrive? Which pressures can collide with the disturbance without becoming filler or mandatory clues?

## Divergent scene engines

Explore enough materially different engines to reveal a genuine choice. Vary the ordinary situation, point of intrusion, human cost, horror mode, clue surface or aftermath—not just wording and furniture.

For each useful engine, note:

- the life current already in motion;
- how the disturbance enters or changes meaning;
- a meaningful choice/cost available to a person;
- what the reader can notice that characters may not assemble;
- the main risk, including plot delivery swallowing the scene.

No fixed number applies. Stop when later options are cosmetic.

## Selection

Choose or combine the strongest engine for human life, horror and fair mystery. State why it was selected and classify it as EXECUTION or AUTHOR-DECISION. Keep rejected engines out of generative context.

## Material open question

Only a change to chapter purpose/outcome, reveal architecture, durable relationship/state, supernatural truth or another consequential lock requires author resolution. Otherwise write `None` and continue autonomously.
