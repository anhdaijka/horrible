> This file is a compressed generative boundary, not a scene outline, dialogue plan or QA checklist.

## Core disturbance and reader experience
State the chapter's central disturbance and the reader experience/question that should remain. Keep this compact. Do not encode a sequence of beats or named craft techniques.

## Hard story boundary
List only the minimum facts that must remain true for the chapter to preserve approved purpose, chronology, reveal edge and durable end state.

Prefer end-state/boundary language over route language.

Bad:
- A tells B X, B reacts Y, then C asks Z.

Better:
- By chapter end, the reader can observe that B recognizes X while A does not know why.

Do not duplicate character knowledge that already belongs in character-context unless it is also a chapter-level reveal boundary.

## Reader-detective field
List only evidence that must remain fairly available. Do not prescribe who asks for it, who comments on it, exact dialogue, exact reaction or the reader's deduction.

## Dramatic space
State what remains open. The writer owns micro-order, dialogue tactics, silence, gesture, local activity, transient interactions and how information emerges inside the hard boundary.

## Concrete reality
Project only practical verified facts the writer truly needs. Keep verification rationale and long exclusions out of generative context.

## Execution freedom
Local events, timing, narrative distance, dialogue length, indirectness, misunderstanding, humor, cooperation and chapter-local life are free unless explicitly locked by the author.

A packet fact is never a required spoken line merely because it appears here.

Record after drafting any new promise, established prior event, custody change, future meeting, distinctive object fact or durable knowledge.

## Deliberately unanswered
Questions the prose may leave implicit or the writer may decide locally. Do not hide an expected answer or QA test here.
