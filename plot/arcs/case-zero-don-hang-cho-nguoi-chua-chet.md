# Case Zero — Đơn hàng cho người chưa chết

## Setup

During a crowded period at the family votive-paper shop, Khanh records a highly specific order intended for a man who is still alive. The purchaser appears to be an ordinary woman and plans to return for the goods.

Several days later, the named man dies after a plausible nighttime fall at home. His family then comes to the shop for funeral goods and appears unaware of the earlier order.

## Stable mystery contract

- The present-day death can remain plausibly accidental.
- A buried family truth from roughly thirty years earlier explains much of the human pressure around the victim.
- A living person deliberately reopens that old matter but does not cleanly explain the shop purchaser.
- The purchaser's identity remains unresolved at case end.
- Human impersonation, a supernatural appearance and a weaker psychological/identity explanation remain viable author-side models.
- Grandfather's old record appears only after most of the human layer is understood and proves only that he encountered the matter in some form.

## Resolution boundary

Khanh learns substantially more but not everything. The case should establish the principle that knowing more is not the same as knowing completely, and that the dead — if present at all — cannot be treated as simple clue-delivery devices.
