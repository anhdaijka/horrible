# Case Zero — Đơn hàng cho người chưa chết

## Core hook

A woman places an ordinary-looking votive-paper order for a named man. At the time of sale, the shop has no reason to discuss whether that man is alive or dead; goods of this kind are naturally understood as intended for the dead unless context says otherwise.

Several days later, the man's family comes to the shop after his unexpected death.

Only then does an earlier administrative detail become frightening: the same man's name is already in the shop's custom-order record, dated **before he died**.

The first mundane explanation is now allowed to arise naturally: perhaps the family knew he was gravely ill and someone prepared the goods in advance.

That explanation weakens only after the shop/family learns that he had been outwardly healthy, living normally and not expected by his household to die.

The order therefore grows more disturbing in layers instead of being announced as strange at purchase time.

## Reader-information rule

Do not let the order scene leak the later twist.

During the purchase:

- nobody asks `ông ấy còn sống à?` merely to set up the premise;
- nobody explains advance purchase for dying people;
- narrator does not reassure the reader that buying for a living person can be normal;
- the purchaser does not volunteer `đặt trước` unless a practical shop detail genuinely requires it;
- the recipient name, date, purchaser identity, left-hand behavior and custom radio may exist as ordinary transaction detail.

The phrase **`đơn hàng cho người chưa chết`** becomes meaningful only retrospectively when the death date is compared against the earlier order date.

## Stable mystery contract

- The present-day death may remain plausibly accidental.
- A buried family truth from roughly thirty years earlier explains much of the human pressure around the victim.
- A living person deliberately reopens that old matter but does not cleanly explain the shop purchaser.
- The purchaser's identity remains unresolved at case end unless later author direction changes this specific case.
- Human impersonation, a supernatural appearance and a weaker psychological/identity explanation remain viable Case Zero models.
- This one case may remain unresolved even though the wider series is no longer ambiguity-by-default.
- Grandfather's old record appears only after most of the human layer is understood and proves only that he encountered the matter in some form.

## Horror contract

The order itself should become frightening through **recontextualization**:

1. ordinary sale;
2. later death;
3. recognition of the name;
4. impossible-looking date relationship;
5. ordinary explanation: expected death / hidden illness;
6. ordinary explanation damaged: victim had appeared healthy and family expected no death;
7. later identity clues make the purchaser herself increasingly disturbing.

Every new layer should make the original sale worse on reread.

## Resolution boundary

Khanh learns substantially more but not everything.

Case Zero should demonstrate that investigation can sharpen horror: the more mundane context Khanh establishes, the more difficult the purchaser becomes to explain.

It must not teach the reader that every ghostly premise will eventually be neutralized.
