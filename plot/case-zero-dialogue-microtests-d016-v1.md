# Case Zero — D-016: Three dialogue micro-tests

Status: D-016 approved the TEST PLAN, not these exercises' future wording. No scenes below are canon prose. See [Voice & Dialogue Bible](case-zero-voice-and-dialogue-bible-v1.md) and [D-015 knowledge boundaries](case-zero-d015-opening-social-and-order-logic.md).

## Shared test brief

Write each test as 450–750 Vietnamese words of natural prose, with a single limited viewpoint and a REAL mundane activity, rather than a script of joke–comeback one-liners. Identify privately what each person wants, knows, fears to lose, and can decide during this exchange. Register must reflect relationship and age without assigning one fixed pronoun to every line. A purposeful short answer is allowed; repetitive generic clipped answers are not. The scene ends with a changed decision, task or personal boundary, not necessarily a quip. Test the draft by hiding speaker tags: if swapping character names works without revision, the voice is insufficiently specific.

### Test 1: bà Lan, Khanh and a younger customer

Context: S01, busy mùng14 at bà Lan's paper-goods shop. A younger customer requests a seven-seat paper car with a genuine pickup constraint and may need to confirm a family preference; the writer must give her her own decision. Bà Lan is occupied by other paid jobs, including a radio for recipient Vũ Văn Cường she does NOT yet link to Lan Anh or a living person at order time. Khanh, 27, just returned from three years onsite Japan for a few weeks' leave, can help mother only within the constraints of a REAL remote-work obligation.

Bà Lan may naturally call Khanh “mày,” “con,” or in an earned annoyed context “anh”; she needn't win every exchange. Khanh can say no to an entire delivery route while carrying out another concrete job. The younger customer should explain her actual deadline where relevant; avoid keeping her as a straight line to enable bà Lan's joke. She may use “cô/cháu” naturally according to age and acquaintance, not because all customers share that voice.

POV: bà Lan. Do not bring in Lan Anh, Long, the cat or supernatural events.

Check: Does each of the three get an independent choice and an appropriate age/relationship register? Remove every punchline: does their conflict and outcome still work?

### Test 2: Lan Anh and Toàn after S03

Context: Lan Anh has just refused in public a radio commissioned on mùng10 by a woman calling herself Vũ Thị Thoa for her father-in-law Vũ Văn Cường, who died mùng13. She learned of the order through bà Lan at Chợ Bến. Lan Anh now tells Toàn that the order came from the family shop of Khanh, the old school junior she once liked. Toàn and Khanh have NEVER met. Toàn has bounded real information that aunt Thoa died and previously used this knowledge to press father for the land deed; Lan Anh does NOT know that old family secret or the full 1996 truth.

POV: Lan Anh limited. Her independent aim is to deal with dangerous gossip while handling practical funeral responsibilities. Toàn has two different anxieties: possible exposure of his family leverage and one-sided jealousy of never-met Khanh. He may ask precise, revealing questions but cannot know what Lan Anh has not told him or turn suspicions into established facts. Lan Anh must make an independent, consequential choice — not merely answer or repeat the purchaser's name. Do not accidentally certify any present affair or ghost knowledge.

Check: Could this scene be replaced by any generic jealous couple without losing meaning? Are his distinct fears legible through actual choices rather than a monologue, and does her own objective survive?

### Test 3: Cổn and Thủy, current household costs

Context: One specific PRESENT household expense or request for Thủy's labor, separate from the long-repaid original wedding borrowing. Cổn has real funeral/household competence and sometimes practical care, alongside his tendency to claim Thủy's service as an endless obligation. Thủy has actual Yên Phong shifts and other wage constraints and has reason to insist on distinguishing current money from historical debt. This is a STANDALONE fictional practice, not a new canon scene or an automatic addition to the mùng13 schedule.

POV: Thủy limited. Cổn may use “mày” to claim authority; it must feel unlike bà Lan's familiar mother–son “mày.” Thủy can respect him while examining one concrete claim, declining a nonessential task or arranging another care solution. Neither must recount all thirty years of family history to the other. Never attribute direct witnessing of Thoa's fatal 1996 blow to Thủy. If placed before G6a, do not borrow Cổn's separately approved post-G6a argument about her genuine modern staged threats.

Check: Does Thủy alter an actual next action? Can you distinguish coercive authority from ordinary familial bluntness through stakes, speech and behavior without relying on insults?

## Review and next gate

Annotate each test on: each speaker's separate objective; observed agency; a sentence or action unique to that person; where register changes and why; whether clipped banter repeats; whether facts/knowledge are chronologically available. Test 1 should fix S01's family/customer voices; tests 2–3 protect against exporting the SAME speaking style to every other character.

Only after reviewing tests should we produce S01 V2 using the ordinary lived-in structure of A-RAW as reference. Original A-RAW and B-RAW remain unmodified NONCANON same-context drafts; the old frozen D-015 experimental prompt and hashes are historical. Any subsequent structured prose trial needs its own run version and honest provenance; none of these test instructions makes sample dialogue approved manuscript.
