## Facts the writer MUST keep separate

1. **Geography and names:** xóm Trại is fictional and substantially distant from busy xóm Bến/Chợ Bến and bà Lan's paper-goods street. A longstanding customer/childhood acquaintance need not know a bride's new father-in-law or family surname. Three distinct people named **Cường** live in xóm Trại: deceased **Vũ Văn Cường** (Toàn's father), one boy presently in **grade seven**, one **78-year-old physically active elder** in the upper lane some distance away. A report about **“ông Cường”** is thus not verified identification of the shop's recipient, much less a reason bà Lan knows the intended recipient's alive/dead status. These are real background conditions, not three plausible named recipients when **full name plus adequate address** are checked.

2. **Bà Lan:** Has long known Lan Anh and knows that Lan Anh was visibly fond of Khanh during **district high-school years**, older than him by one or two school cohorts, later graduated university and unexpectedly married Toàn into an xóm Trại household with an elderly grandfather/patriarch **Cổn** famous for rigid, authoritarian family control. Even bà Lan **does NOT know** her old acquaintance's father-in-law is named **Vũ Văn Cường**, and does not know the marital family's surname **Vũ**. On **mùng10 lunar July 2026 (22 Aug)** a woman giving her name as Thoa genuinely enters the shop, left-handedly revises a particular hand-drawn detail for an authentic custom **red Czech cassette-radio paper replica**, names/records **Vũ Văn Cường** as recipient and PREPAYS. It is an ordinary legitimate order among a busy run-up to rằm; the customer need not volunteer whether it is meant for a living person, fresh death, ancestral ceremony or annual memorial. Bà Lan's occupational competence is practical order/payment/time/pickup information, not clairvoyance or intrusive death verification. Objective D-012 author truth only: Cường was alive mùng10; true ghost Thoa commissioned his planned funeral gift.

3. **Khanh:** Age ~27, attended the district high school (Lan Anh 1–2 classes/cohorts senior). Worked **1–2 years at a Japanese company** in Vietnam, then **3 years onsite in Japan**, has **only just returned** at novel opening for **a few weeks' holiday** at home and expects to return to the company's **inner-Hanoi office**. Still has real remote meetings/handover obligations for the Japanese business during holiday; his presence in mother's shop is intermittent and practical. He knows Lan Anh formerly liked him and her later marriage as local history; **do not** infer he is in a secret renewed romance or deeply informed about her husband's family. He and Toàn **have not met or interacted face to face** prior to story time.

4. **Lan Anh:** In school 1–2 cohorts above Khanh and once liked him intensely enough the story spread locally. Her decision to marry Toàn after university does not prove she still loves Khanh, lied about a relationship or introduced Khanh to her husband's secrets. She has real mourning responsibilities; on mùng14 (26 Aug) her independent funeral-related errands make a **normal encounter** at Chợ Bến possible. She did NOT know what happened to Thoa in 1996, did not see the Toàn–Cường shove and must not become a conduit of unsupported hidden knowledge. Her public refusal of the prepaid radio stays D-005/D-011/14 canon; after she reports the name at home, Toàn's special reaction can be shown **at his own limited knowledge level**.

5. **Toàn:** About thirty, has heard gossip about Khanh's school success/Japanese career and Lan Anh's intense adolescent affection for a junior one or two grades below her. He and Khanh had **never directly met** in earlier years, so jealousy is a **one-sided imagined comparison**. Toàn has acquired a **bounded piece of information that his aunt Thoa really DIED**; source and degree of knowledge still need a later earned scene. He **strategically used this partial fact to threaten/pressure his father** in his attempt to obtain the land-use deed (**sổ đỏ**) under real trucking/speculation debts. Cường's refusal and the actual mùng13 fight remain independent human events; possessing/pressing for the physical deed does not automatically confer a legal right in land. AFTER Lan Anh reports the radio order at Khanh family's shop, Toàn combines fear that his old leverage/1996 secret may be exposed with jealous suspicion toward the academically successful man his wife once loved; he grows guarded, sometimes overtly controlling toward Khanh **without evidence Khanh knows the hidden murder or reciprocates a current romance**. First face-to-face Khanh–Toàn interaction in the novel needs grounded placement as a **new** encounter, not a reunion.

### S03 logistics must be preflighted, not assumed

If the customer instructed **pickup at the shop**, why is the radio carried past Lan Anh at the market on mùng14? The writer must give bà Lan a normal delivery/errand reason (e.g. a customer-confirmed market handoff or routine delivery bag with expected pickup) that **existed at sale time**, rather than inventing recipient's home address at the moment of the public confrontation. The author approved the **principle of identifying recipient naturally on S03 by Lan Anh's information and independent disambiguation**, not an arbitrary exact contact method. Establish one consistent order slip (name, requested craft/repair detail, date, agreed handoff/contact, deposit/payment) and keep it unchanged across S01/S03/S04/S07, including who retains original and who may show it. Full name Vũ Văn Cường identifies more than 'Cường' alone but does not by itself prove whether he's Lan Anh's father-in-law without her confirmation or independent record.

### One emotional payoff requiring no author miracle

Early bà Lan and Khanh are dealing with a weird **subsequently discovered** professional/social collision, not an omniscient detective thriller; meanwhile Toàn hears not just **“my aunt Thoa signed a death-gift for my living father”** but also **“she went through the family shop of the man my wife once adored and who may have returned from Japan.”** The second fact is a **belief-laden social trigger** for the husband and never proof of an existing affair. Two people may talk past each other in their FIRST encounter because Khanh is trying to protect his mother’s honest transaction while Toàn is trying to protect a family secret and control a marriage. Sustain normal work, money, funeral labor and family awkwardness between investigative beats.
