# Case Zero — Chợ Bến / Yên Phong independent mini-arc packets

**2026-09-26. D-011 approved the V1 treatment's two independently meaningful side-story STRANDS. The specific people, losses, incidents and resolutions here are SCENE PROPOSALS, not fixed author canon or official socioeconomic research.** They should not become a didactic relay where every subplot exists to teach the reader unreliable testimony.

## A. Chợ Bến: A cart that arrived too late

**Lived protagonist:** a market trader *temporarily* named Nhàn runs morning wholesale deliveries while sharing canopy and a covered delivery cart with Hảo, another seller in a crowded fictional market corner. The shared cart is not evidence for the Cường investigation; its immediate stakes are Nhàn's vanishing customer credit and Hảo's inability to pay a real share of stall maintenance.

**Episode 1, before the radio collision (S02):** Nhàn has an actual advance order for a wholesale delivery to a distant customer; if she misses a van window she loses profit and supplier reputation, not her life. Hảo had borrowed the cart and genuinely returned it to the customary spot, but she had failed to pay her share for the canopy repair, providing an existing interpersonal reason Nhàn interprets every delay as selfishness. A drainage/road worker relocates the cart because of a practical street-clearing problem before Nhàn arrives. Nobody stands in the scene to recite a lesson about witness memory. **Scene-ending change:** Nhàn misses the first handoff, tries to get a substitute transport arrangement, and leaves the market crossing emotionally/physically unsettled. When bà Lan and Khanh later bring the paper radio, the two traders happen to have separate ordinary reasons to be present. The mùng14 public refusal occurs in another scene and adds gossip traffic; it does not solve or create the cart dispute.

**Episode 2, after public refusal (S15):** Nhàn attempts to reclaim the lost supplier arrangement and discovers a person saw Hảo leave the cart in one position; she believes this vindicates her account only if the witness saw the entire later interval. A separate drainage-worker/driver record establishes the relocation for work but the missing supplier slot cannot be recovered. Bà Lan has a shop delivery delay of her own and resents being dragged into the traders' quarrel. Khanh hears enough to know the two are fighting about **a real late delivery**, not a ghost-case analogy. **Scene-ending change:** cart-location question resolved; Hảo's unpaid repair share remains the difficult negotiation.

**Episode 3, independent closure (S33/S39):** Hảo pays part of the old canopy share or agrees to a dated pay plan backed by a real sale, not a neat apology that repairs the lost wholesale order. Nhàn gets a different smaller customer with worse margins and decides to stop sharing the cart at peak morning hours. Their relationship settles into workable dislike; market gossip about the radio is no longer their whole existence. The tale would still have conflict, cost and ending if Case Zero's murder were entirely removed.

**Guardrails:** Names Hảo/Nhàn remain tentative. Do not identify traders as criminal witnesses through contrived surveillance, give them motive knowledge of 1996 or put the case's key discovery in their carts. If the cart subplot feels too tidy/lesson-like, move emphasis to supplier deadlines and shared-stall debt.

## B. Yên Phong: The rework batch that counts for nobody

**Lived protagonist:** coworker *temporarily* named Phượng at a **fictional** garment workshop, with an ordinary pay dispute over how corrected defects count under a company-specific piecework/bonus or performance-accounting policy. Exact payroll law, hours, employer rules and worker status need verification before detailed legal claims. Phượng wants credit for labor actually performed; money matters for a personal bill but her story is not merely "the worker who happened to see Thủy."

**Episode 1, a week before death (S08):** a batch inspection marks multiple sewn items defective even though different people worked on initial assembly. The workshop requests rework but the group cannot get a clear written agreement about whether corrected pieces count toward individual performance bonuses. Some colleagues want to work and avoid conflict; Phượng wants the error and extra labor documented, and Thủy has developed her own habit of recording money she sends or work she completes. Neither needs to talk about dead Thoa, village ghosts or a future homicide; Phượng is more irritated by a manager who changes calculation terms midstream than she is by Thủy's silence.

**Episode 2, the actual mùng13 night shift (S17, later told through Phượng's finite POV if early Thủy inner truth would leak):** Phượng and Thủy perform real side-by-side rework **through the author-only ~22:54 time**, starting after a normal 22:00 shift handover. Another lead actually visits for a sample check during an overlapping segment; independently logged work movements are useful only with identity and clock corroboration. Phượng keeps her own note because of the pay dispute, **not** because the author needs an alibi notebook. Thủy learns of Cường's death AFTER the fatal window, speaks to supervisor, is permitted to leave and passes out through recorded exit; don't invent a message physically sent by her at 22:54 while continuously working. **Scene-ending change:** Phượng must cover part of a real unfinished batch and becomes angrier about uncompensated rework, while worrying about a colleague who just lost a family member.

**Episode 3, independent closure (S33/S39):** when the management dispute actually has to be resolved, Phượng decides whether she will sign an amended shift/rework sheet that records what she personally did while refusing a false statement about what she cannot know. A partial pay correction might come through ordinary workplace discussion; if it fails she can seek next realistic steps, but the story's emotional outcome is her ability to set boundaries with Thủy and the lead. Her direct observation later helps corroborate Thủy's alibi; it does NOT make her witness every action or a moral arbiter of 1996.

**Guardrails:** Do not imply real company misconduct, use a real employer logo or treat a clock-in scan as continuous eyewitness proof. Keep invented policy explicitly fictional. No need to name a legal union/complaints authority without 2026 research. Phượng may be sympathetic to Thủy and still resent pressure on her own job and reputation.
