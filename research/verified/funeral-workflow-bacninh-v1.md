# Verified Research — Contemporary Funeral Workflow in Bắc Ninh / Northern Vietnam v1

Status: verified research note, not story canon.

Scope: practical guardrails for writing a contemporary death/funeral sequence in the `Horrible` opening region. This is **not** a universal ritual template for all Northern Vietnamese families.

## High-confidence legal / administrative frame

### 1. Current Bắc Ninh policy explicitly recognizes multiple modern funeral pathways

Bắc Ninh's current regulation on civilized practice in weddings/funerals (Decision 116/2025/QĐ-UBND, effective 12 January 2026) requires funeral organization to be dignified, economical and compliant with health/environmental law. It explicitly refers to:

- khâm liệm;
- quàn / quàn ướp;
- transport;
- burial / mai táng;
- cremation / hỏa táng;
- funeral homes / nhà tang lễ;
- cremation centres.

It encourages cremation/electric cremation and modern funeral facilities rather than assuming burial at home as the only normal form.

Sources:
- Bắc Ninh provincial portal, 05/01/2026: https://bacninh.gov.vn/news/-/details/37632/quy-inh-thuc-hien-nep-song-van-minh-trong-viec-cuoi-viec-tang-va-mot-so-sinh-hoat-cong-ong-84489901
- official legal database copy: https://vbpl.vn/bacninh/Pages/vbpq-toanvan.aspx?ItemID=186464

Confidence: high.

Story implication:
- a semi-urbanizing family may use a funeral service, funeral home, cremation centre or a mixed home/service workflow;
- do not write the hometown as frozen in an old village funeral system;
- local custom can coexist with professional services and state regulation.

### 2. Cremation is institutionally encouraged and materially supported in contemporary Bắc Ninh

Bắc Ninh's Resolution 107/2025/NQ-HĐND provides support of 10 million VND per cremation/electric-cremation case from 01/01/2026.

Source:
- Bắc Ninh Department of Culture, 31/12/2025: https://svhttdl.bacninh.gov.vn/news/-/details/188850/bac-ninh-ban-hanh-nghi-quyet-moi-ve-ho-tro-chi-phi-hoa-tang-thu-tuc-gon-hon-thuan-tien-hon-cho-nguoi-dan-83705383

Confidence: high.

Story implication:
- cremation should feel socially ordinary/plausible in the 2026-equivalent setting, not like an urban outsider practice;
- older relatives may still prefer burial/cát táng while younger relatives prefer cremation, giving credible family disagreement without supernatural cause.

### 3. National health law defines practical constraints on quàn and khâm liệm

Ministry of Health Circular 21/2021/TT-BYT remains in force. It defines `quàn` as keeping/preserving the body before burial/cremation and `khâm liệm` as procedures for transferring the body into the coffin.

For a death not caused by dangerous epidemic disease:

- without refrigeration, body keeping is limited to 48 hours from death;
- with refrigeration at 4°C or below, up to 7 days;
- khâm liệm itself is performed according to local custom, belief and religion, while hygiene/sealing requirements still apply.

Sources:
- Ministry of Health / national legal database: https://vbpl.vn/boyte/Pages/vbpq-toanvan.aspx?ItemID=152953&Keyword=
- national legal database: https://vbpl.vn/TW/Pages/vbpq-toanvan.aspx?ItemID=152953

Confidence: high.

Story implication:
- after the Case Zero victim dies, funeral preparation begins quickly for practical reasons;
- it is entirely plausible for relatives to need funeral goods the same day or next morning;
- do not let the corpse remain at home for several unrefrigerated days merely to create atmosphere.

## High-confidence cultural frame: variation is normal

### 4. There is no single universal contemporary Northern funeral sequence

Research on funerals in the Northern Delta shows variation by religion, family, locality, generation and modernization. An ethnographic/doctoral study of Northern Delta funeral practices includes Bắc Ninh among its field sites and separately tracks Buddhist, Catholic and non-religious Vietnamese practice, including differences in post-funeral rites, votive-paper use, burial forms and feast scale.

Source lead:
- Lê Thị Cúc, research on funeral rites of Northern Vietnamese Buddhist/Catholic communities; accessible summary: https://www.zbook.vn/ebook/luan-an-tang-thuc-cua-nguoi-viet-bac-bo-la-tin-do-phat-giao-va-cong-giao-54123/

Confidence: high for variation; use the thesis/book itself when a scene needs specific numbers or detailed ritual sequence.

### 5. Contemporary rural funerals combine continuity with reform/professional services

Current cultural research on suburban/northern rural funeral practice describes shorter organization, reduced large-scale feasting in many places, growing involvement of professional funeral services, and coexistence of state `nếp sống văn minh` policy with retained family/local ritual meaning.

Source:
- Institute for Cultural Studies (VASS), 20/01/2026: https://vncvanhoa.vass.gov.vn/nghien-cuu-64215/su-thich-ung-cua-nguoi-dan-voi-tieu-chi-nong-thon-moi-nhin-tu-thuc-hanh-tang-ma-o-ngoai-thanh-ha-noi-552012

Confidence: medium-high for regional modernization pattern.

### 6. Burial practice in the Northern Delta is changing

Recent ethnology research documents movement in parts of the Northern Delta from older `hung táng → cát táng` patterns toward cremation and one-time burial.

Source:
- Nguyễn Thị Thanh Bình, `Chuyển đổi trong nhận thức về mai táng của người Việt ở đồng bằng Bắc Bộ hiện nay`: https://vjol.info.vn/tapchiviendantochoc/vi/article/view/110579/

Confidence: high for broad transition, not for one specific village.

## Buddhist-influenced funeral vocabulary — usable only when the family context supports it

A National Museum of History overview describes a Buddhist-influenced Vietnamese sequence containing terms such as:

- nhập liệm;
- phát tang;
- tiến linh / cúng cơm;
- cầu siêu;
- cáo tổ tiên before di quan;
- di quan / hạ huyệt;
- post-funeral prayer/cúng in seven weekly periods up to 49 days.

Source:
- National Museum of History: https://baotanglichsuquocgia.vn/vi/Articles/3091/13843/phat-giao-trong-djoi-song-nguoi-viet-nam.html

Confidence: medium-high as a Buddhist-influenced overview.

Do not infer:
- every Northern family performs all these rites;
- all terms are used identically in every locality;
- a family using Buddhist prayers accepts all surrounding folk beliefs;
- 49-day practice implies votive burning is Buddhist doctrine.

## What this means for Case Zero

A realistic present-day sequence can safely be built as follows **without yet locking ritual minutiae**:

1. the man dies at home at night after a fall;
2. emergency/medical/police handling depends on circumstances of death;
3. family confirms death and begins administrative/funeral arrangements;
4. funeral-service / family network begins practical preparation quickly;
5. relatives may visit Khanh's shop for ordinary goods within the first day or next morning;
6. khâm liệm / quàn / funeral ceremony proceed according to the fictional family's chosen local/religious pattern;
7. burial versus cremation remains a family-specific story decision, not a regional default.

This is enough to make the return of the dead man's family to the shop plausible without inventing a `standard Northern funeral package`.

## Still requires research before writing close-up ritual scenes

- exact role of funeral-service staff versus family elders in the chosen fictional locality;
- exact mourning clothes/tang phục for this family;
- local sound/music practices;
- whether monks are invited and what they actually do;
- exact sequence/timing of phát tang, nhập quan, viếng, di quan;
- food/meal scale;
- procession details;
- burial/cremation destination;
- post-funeral altar arrangement;
- family-specific 3-day / 49-day / 100-day observances.

## Hard drafting rule

Use the verified legal/modernity frame freely. Treat the exact ritual sequence as family/locality-specific until separately researched.
