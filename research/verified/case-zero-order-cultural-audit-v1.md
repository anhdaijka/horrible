# Case Zero Cultural Audit v1 — `Đơn hàng cho người chưa chết`

Status: research-grounded adaptation audit. **Author-approved and incorporated into the accepted Case Zero Truth Matrix.** Still research evidence, not Story Skills canon by itself.

Purpose: preserve the mystery/clue design while removing accidental claims that invented shop procedure is a universal Vietnamese ritual rule.

## New P0 finding: `hàng mã for a living person` is not automatically impossible

Paper effigies / `hình nhân thế mạng` and related folk practices can concern a living person in cầu an / giải hạn / substitution contexts. Reporting and religious commentary both document the category even while Buddhist voices criticize the belief.

Sources:
- VOV on Phúc Am production, including `hình nhân thế mạng`: https://vov.vn/the-thao/lang-nghe-chi-song-voi-ngay-am-post1163916.vov
- VnExpress interview discussing `hình nhân thế mạng cho người sống`: https://vnexpress.net/su-thay-thich-minh-quang-thang-7-am-lich-dep-nhat-nam-3967241.html
- Nhân Dân 2025 on `hình nhân bản mệnh` and differentiated mã traditions: https://nhandan.vn/bac-vang-hang-ma-ky-1-post906571.html

Conclusion:

> Case Zero cannot rely on `paper goods + living person's name` alone to mean `funeral order for a living man`.

The order must be **death/memorial-coded in its stated purpose or product context** while remaining ordinary enough that Lan initially suspects mistaken identity, duplicate name, advance family planning or customer confusion rather than supernatural prediction.

## What remains culturally plausible and can be kept

### A. A highly specific personalized paper-goods order

Supported by modern custom-production reporting.

Keep.

### B. An old-style paper radio / cassette-radio as the memorable item

Paper radios have appeared in Vietnamese gold-paper retail reporting, and current Song Hồ production supports many modeled consumer/personal objects.

Sources:
- radio explicitly mentioned in retail reporting: https://soha.vn/xa-hoi/ram-thang-7-dich-vu-chuyen-tien-xuong-ca-coi-am-20140808044527122.htm
- current/broader Song Hồ modeled-object reporting: https://vnexpress.net/biet-thu-oto-iphone-vang-ma-cung-ram-thang-7-3268443.html

Keep, but write it as an unusual personal/custom request, not a ritual category with special mystical meaning.

### C. Cash payment and later pickup

Ordinary commerce. Keep.

### D. Customer plans to return in three days

Possible as a customer-requested deadline, but **not verified as a normal custom-item lead time**.

Keep only as family/supplier-specific fiction. Lan may say it is tight, or simply say she has to check with the maker. The accepted fact only requires that the purchaser intends to return in three days; the shop does not need to guarantee an industry-standard three-day turnaround.

### E. Victim's relatives coming to the shop shortly after death

Strongly plausible. National health rules require practical funeral preparation to move quickly, and current Bắc Ninh rules explicitly recognize khâm liệm/quàn/mai táng/hỏa táng workflows.

Keep.

## What should NOT remain narrator-implied ritual law

### 1. `Full name + lunar age + address are mandatory for a votive order`

Not verified.

Low-confidence ritual websites claim various name/age/address inscriptions, but the evidence is too weak and inconsistent to make this a Northern cultural rule.

### 2. `A fixed standard funeral/votive package always contains X/Y/Z`

Not verified.

Commercial bundles exist, but product mix varies by shop, occasion, family, tradition and contemporary consumption.

### 3. `Three days is a normal custom manufacturing lead time`

Not verified.

Elaborate modern custom shops advertise longer lead times; a simple radio may be faster, but this should remain story-specific logistics.

## Approved minimal-change version of the order

This preserves almost every existing clue while fixing the cultural logic.

### Stated purpose

The woman asks for **male paper goods in a memorial/deceased-recipient context for a named recipient**, plus one unusual personal item: an old-style cassette radio.

The prose does not need Lan to use a formal product phrase like `bộ mã giỗ chuẩn`. Natural wording can establish the purpose through context:

- she says it is `làm cho` the named man;
- she speaks as though the goods will be sent/burned for him as a deceased recipient;
- she selects ordinary male paper clothing/personal goods and asks for the radio.

Because the recipient is locally known to be alive, Lan later reacts: either the customer has the wrong person, there are two people with the same name, or something about the order is wrong.

### Ordinary goods — keep flexible for now

Do **not** lock a ritual package yet.

Safe current candidates based on observed retail categories:

- one male / older-male paper clothing set;
- paper shoes or personal accessories;
- an ordinary amount of paper money/gold-paper stock;
- the custom old-style radio.

These are individual retail items, not a narrator-certified `funeral bundle`.

### Two identities must be separated on the order record

For fair-play clarity, the shop record distinguishes:

**A. `Người đặt` / pickup identity**
- the woman gives the name that later belongs to the dead third sibling;
- phone number may be absent/refused because she says she will return herself.

**B. `Làm cho ai` / intended recipient**
- full name of the living male victim;
- enough identifying detail to distinguish him from a namesake.

This separation makes the later reveal cleaner than one unexplained block of names.

## Approved treatment of recipient age/address

### Full name — KEEP

Natural for a personalized/custom order and important for plot.

### Age / `tuổi âm` — KEEP ONLY AS CUSTOMER-PROVIDED DETAIL, NOT SHOP REQUIREMENT

The purchaser may arrive with a handwritten slip and give an unusually precise age/year detail. Khanh copies it because she gave it, not because all votive shops require it.

Alternative later if stronger scene realism suggests it: use birth year / approximate age rather than explicitly `tuổi âm`.

### Address — KEEP AS DISAMBIGUATION / FAMILY-SHOP ADMIN DETAIL

Use the locality/house address because:

- it distinguishes which Nguyễn Văn X she means;
- the shop sometimes records delivery/locality for custom orders;
- or the purchaser voluntarily wrote it on her slip.

Do not tell the reader this is required so `the dead can receive the goods`.

## Approved order-taking choreography

This is a prose design, not ethnographic transcript:

1. shop is crowded;
2. woman asks for several ordinary male paper goods;
3. asks for an old-style cassette radio with very specific physical details;
4. Khanh asks who the goods are for because he needs to write the custom/pickup note;
5. she gives/slides over recipient details;
6. Khanh recognizes nothing immediately because he has been away / local names are ordinary;
7. for pickup, he asks the purchaser's name and possibly phone;
8. she gives the dead sister's name but no useful phone number, saying she will return in three days;
9. only later Lan notices the recipient is a living local man.

This creates the uncanny through **ordinary commercial friction**, not ritual exposition.

## Why the radio still works as fair-play clue

The radio should encode private family memory:

- old rectangular cassette/radio form;
- unusual arrangement of speaker/tuning/cassette details;
- perhaps a color or repaired feature remembered from the victim's family.

Important rule:

> the shop/maker can construct the object from the purchaser's description without needing to know why that exact radio matters.

Later, the family recognizes the described object as something associated with the dead third sibling / old household history.

Keep at least one living transmission route viable so the clue does not prove a ghost.

## Death-to-funeral-shop timing recommendation

Current law/practice makes a quick return plausible.

Recommended rhythm:

- death occurs at night;
- by the next morning/day, family/funeral-service logistics are already underway;
- someone from the family comes to Lan's shop for ordinary funeral/memorial paper goods;
- Lan recognizes the deceased name/address from the earlier custom order.

No need for the victim family to buy an elaborate `complete package`; a practical small purchase is enough to trigger recognition.

## Chapter 1 cultural carryover

The prior Chapter 1 shorthand `loại năm mươi hay loại tám mươi` had no verified referent and should not survive manuscript approval. Replace it with ordinary, context-readable retail dialogue rather than inventing a shop code.

The dream-about-tight-sleeve scene can remain as family-specific retail/customization texture if the novel does not generalize Lan's behavior into a regional ritual rule.

## Author decisions — approved

The author approved all five adaptation decisions:

1. separate `người đặt` versus `làm cho ai` in the shop ledger;
2. make death/memorial-coded purpose necessary for the order's disturbing meaning;
3. treat `tuổi âm` and address as purchaser-provided / shop-admin details rather than ritual requirements;
4. keep the three-day return as a requested deadline, not an industry norm;
5. leave the exact ordinary product list flexible until the Chapter 2 brief rather than inventing a fixed funeral package.

These decisions are now incorporated into `development/horrible/case-zero-truth-matrix.md`.
