# Case Zero — Supporting v1: autonomous mini-arcs, concrete late climax, candid design audit

**2026-09-26 — DEVELOPMENT OPTIONS, NOT AUTHOR-APPROVED NEW FACTS.** Read with [07 Volume Treatment V1](07_VOLUME_TREATMENT_V1.md) and [08 chronology/horror ledger](08_VOLUME_EVENT_AND_KNOWLEDGE_LEDGER_V1.md). This annex concretizes the weak sections of V1 rather than claiming they are resolved. Author must approve any concrete new participants, details, horror phenomena, injury and final Cổn behavior before they become canon.

## 1. Chợ Bến — "một buổi chợ, ba bên cùng thiệt" (full candidate mini-arc)

**Own dramatic premise:** Road/drainage work temporarily changes access to a small part of the fictional market. Two sellers share a canopy and some heavy carrying equipment under an informal arrangement. Tạm tên **Hảo**, seller of household goods, arranged to borrow a covered delivery cart from **Nhàn**, who handles early-morning deliveries and is herself under pressure to make a wholesale drop. A third person (hired to clear a drain overnight) relocated the cart after moving goods from a waterlogged area. Hảo genuinely did return the cart to the agreed place at a time she can remember; Nhàn genuinely lost a sale and wrongly attributes deliberate retaliation to Hảo. Importantly, Hảo also **was late paying her portion of canopy maintenance**, which is a separate, real contribution to the quarrel.

**Beginning:** The dispute is already live when bà Lan and Khanh bring a finished paper radio to Chợ Bến on morning mùng14. Traders waiting for the cart are not silent background "witnesses"; they have a stake in the quarrel and repeatedly interrupt and misunderstand the radio refusal. **Radio incident canon** stays public and the object returns to the shop; rumor escapes along a trader's actual supply route to someone who later talks with Long.

**Middle:** Hảo and Nhàn separately appeal to different traders; a simple observation ("sáng nay bà ấy kéo xe về phía đó") spreads as proof of motive, although the observer did not see who moved the cart afterward. Khanh does not become a judge of everybody's credibility. His mother loses a small but meaningful order because shop deliveries are caught in the queue and rumor. A practical account/driver testimony resolves **where** the cart moved; the underlying unpaid canopy cost still requires Hảo to own a real fault. Both women lose something they cannot magically recover, but market work goes on.

**End:** Nhàn regains the cart and renegotiates shared equipment instead of receiving a polished courtroom apology. Hảo pays or contests her real share and has to rebuild supplier confidence. The mini-arc's last scene concerns an ordinary delayed delivery, not the Cường case. If it feels like an instructional parallel about unreliable witnesses, *de-emphasize* the parallel and let the unpaid maintenance and supplier stakes dominate.

**Full-story test:** if all mentions of Cường, Thoa and the paper radio were removed, readers could still care about whether Hảo keeps her stall and whether Nhàn loses her wholesale customer. If not, do not grant this strand several standalone scenes.

## 2. Yên Phong — "ca không được tính" (full candidate mini-arc)

**Own premise:** Coworker tạm tên **Phượng** is assigned a batch requiring rework at a fictitious garment workshop. Some completed pieces were logged as defects then corrected but the overtime/rework allocation in payroll omits labor by several operators. A team leader worries that escalating the accounting dispute will expose a procedural mistake. Phượng wants proper pay and recognition, partly for ordinary rent/family commitments, not because she senses a future murder investigation.

**Beginning:** In the week before mùng13, show Phượng and Thủy at work as skilled but temperamentally different women. Thủy has a direct interest in getting her own past payments recognized, which colors how she argues with Phượng about keeping a private wage notebook. The pay conflict starts BEFORE Cường's death; don't invent it after police need a witness.

**Middle:** During the mùng13 night shift, they actually perform a quality/rework task together over an interval that **includes the author-only ~22:54 anchor**; other overlapping sources include an in-person production lead with partial direct sight and work-system events with independently checked identity/clock. Each source is fallible. Phượng may initially hesitate to testify if the employer suggests logs were edited to repair payroll; her own fight for fair pay provides a coherent reason she retained separate contemporaneous notes. Avoid forcing the supervisor to falsify murder evidence for mere wages.

**End:** Phượng pursues a limited correction in the shop's pay records and decides which colleagues she can trust. Thủy may have to accept she has endangered someone else's work reputation by relying on their support in an unrelated investigation, so she owes Phượng something concrete beyond thanks. Employer policy/union/complaint procedure requires 2026 research before detailed legal narration. The women remain colleagues after the murder case's immediate purpose ends; they have agency outside Cường.

**Full-story test:** Phượng's wage outcome and Thủy's work relationship would matter even if nobody examined the death.

## 3. Ordinary shop and engineer plots — retain one, avoid making all subplots symbolic

**Independent votive-shop commission candidate:** Two surviving relatives disagree over a modest custom offering for a loved one; bà Lan refuses to alter a paid order when only one person demands it without consulting the other. Their conflict is specific to the person's actual hobby and how different relatives remembered them, NOT another foreshadowed murder or spirit-identification card. End in an awkward ordinary business compromise that may leave one party dissatisfied. If this mirrors Thoa's radio too neatly, cut it and simply show ongoing shop orders/late invoices without a full mini-arc.

**Independent Khanh software incident:** He must handle a production incident for a Japan-facing client where an ordinary session-to-account mapping fault misattributes a user action. It threatens a colleague's review unless Khanh checks an edge case; his own earlier rushed approval contributed to the production change. Solve through authorized logs and collaborative review, with after-hours cost to Khanh's trip home. He **does not** use company telemetry or code to decrypt Cường's police-held phone or access unrelated private records. Resolution should happen mid-to-late book while the supernatural case remains open: the technician can close *one work ticket* but not close the existence of a ghost.

**Story load:** promote Chợ Bến and Yên Phong to independent mini-arcs first. Keep the shop/engineering strands as recurring ordinary pressure; promote to full B-plot only if narrative space truly demands it.

## 4. H4 — last house crisis with a real reason for everybody to be present (concrete test)

V1 originally left the climax's practical trigger underspecified. Try the following **OPTION H4-A**, but do NOT lock its precise ghost forms or any new Cổn misdeed before explicit author acceptance.

**Entry after G6a and major G6b:** Lan Anh has resolved to spend time apart from Toàn following his attempt to make her corroborate an event she did not witness. She returns at a **prearranged daylight time** to collect her own work/seasonal belongings from the house rather than wander back at midnight to get paranormal clues. Thủy chooses to accompany her, in part as reciprocal human trust after they each told uncomfortable truths. Toàn turns up because he fears the marriage and the family house are slipping away. Cổn still lives in / refuses to abandon the house out of long-standing family identity and because he genuinely lacks good alternatives. This naturally places four characters in a familiar space without a new conspiracy or an urgent magical summons.

**Opening of event:** The belongings are not in a convenient box by the gate; they must separate what belongs to Lan Anh from goods she has managed for the family. A dispute flares over an ordinary possession/record. Because Cổn's new 2026 wrong is intentionally unapproved, **do not predecide whether he orders anything destroyed or physically blocks a witness**. He can manifest his already established need to retain authority through plain speech, the allocation of tasks and refusal to admit that Lan Anh may leave.

**First inversion:** Movement of an everyday load within the house causes an unmistakably extraordinary barrier *or* bodily threat that cannot be reproduced by Thủy's expired fake-audio scheme. One of Lan Anh/Thủy is physically trapped, and Toàn realizes he can either keep trying to control what everyone says about him or help immediately. Avoid repeating H1's empty-bed weight trick as final spectacle. The ghost should be perceived through a DIFFERENT channel here: a close, direct, bodily Cường encounter and material danger to a living person in a confined room, with the grossly abnormal neck remaining one possible unsettling body image but not the whole scare.

**Escalation:** Two characters outside the immediate room hear/see only what their positions permit. Cổn recognizes his dead son not as a moral policeman but as a man who, after death, still wants to command the very spaces Cổn taught him to dominate. Toàn can be frightened and still choose to help his wife, without pretending the shirt was never destroyed. Thủy needs to make a physical rescue choice from *ordinary skills and a disabled left hand she lives with*, not a mystical ability unlocked by trauma.

**Exit and cost:** They get the endangered person out by a concrete physical maneuver that will be staged in the preflight after the house geography is fixed, not by saying Thoa's name as a spell. A real injury, damage to personal possessions or having to leave the house structurally unsafe must survive into following chapters; **do not introduce an additional named-character death without author approval**. Ghost Cường has a last observable withdrawal/cessation as required by canon, but avoid claiming his disappearance is an automatic reward for truth-telling, forgiveness or one successful ritual. The family then needs a real future answer to who lives where and who will care for Cổn.

**Stronger horror branch H4-B to test only if A is too mild:** Cổn's own long-standing domestic duty attracts a separately staged violent apparition BEFORE the final group event; he is physically incapable of dismissing it in public, but family remains skeptical of what he saw. Final H4-A then escalates to a multiple-witness close encounter with materially different perceptions. The chronology must ensure G6a/b earlier enough that a reader already understands who Thoa was; do not use her as an invisible fair referee who saves them.

## 5. V1 adversarial critique — what currently does NOT yet earn a long novel

1. **H1/H2/H4 are structural horror suggestions, not guaranteed terrifying prose.** Heavy furniture and momentary disorientation can turn into stock haunted-house effects if scenes are abstract. In the next pass physically diagram kitchen/room/doors, household job, bodily exposure, exit constraints and post-event logistics. If an effect looks derivative, invent a genuinely different one; do not recycle old archived manifestations.
2. **Bình's B-I bluff still risks being conveniently effective.** The first probe must be shown in a lived scene that also does something ordinary for Bình. Readers should understand why Cường fears even a narrow disclosure and why Bình believes a second demand will work. One dramatic reaction alone is not a murder confession.
3. **T-H1b still contains one unconstrained accounting/photo choice.** A phone photo, torn sheet or hidden receipt may be too easy to plant. The visit only needs a real moral conflict, concealment and finite trace; if the photograph feels authored solely for forensic recovery, drop it. Thủy's false threats already give her enough moral culpability.
4. **The 1996 loan and 2026 property outcome are not yet economically modeled.** Need a dated table of original loan, repayments, successor household costs, who controlled which use rights and whether the family's current land expectation is legally/financially plausible. Don't award Thủy a plot-magic title simply because she's morally owed compensation.
5. **Toàn knows Thoa dead and pre-G6a POV is risky.** Early focalization belongs mostly to Lan Anh, bà Lan/Khanh, other ordinary witnesses and limited Long, not an artificially forgetful Toàn/Thủy/Cổn. Reader may guess early but narrator cannot affirm her death too soon.
6. **The two autonomous side stories are not yet dramatic scenes.** They now have beginning/conflict/end and costs, but characters and places still lack voices and relationship texture. In next scene map create an episode of each where Cường's case is not even mentioned.
7. **Cổn's unchosen 2026 act leaves an intentional hole in final escalation.** Do not fill it by inventing a villainous arson, false testimony, bodily injury, ritual with guaranteed effects, or additional death. First earn his character through three relational tests and then have author decide an actionable final wrong or restraint.
8. **G6c position can deflate H4 if placed after final climax as an expository final twist.** Trial strongest order: G6a identification → staged G6b human reconstruction → restrained G6c reader confirmation of Thoa → late independent Cường haunting climax, followed by lived aftermath. This lets the book continue to terrify after its foundational mystery is knowable. Split the short G6c confirmation only if literary execution benefits.
9. **Seasonal month seven may be too on-the-nose.** Its strongest real use is high seasonal shop demand and public crowd dynamics; if readers feel the supernatural must follow a ghost-month timer, choose an earlier rainy month instead without violating mùng10/13/14 relative dates.
10. **No hard length should be set yet.** A manuscript-size estimate must be driven by number of real autonomous episodes, horror consequences, delayed social fallout and multi-week procedural chronology, not a chapter quota.
