# NGƯỜI CÒN Ở NHÀ — INTERNAL SPOILERS

2026-10-09. Local experimental design under the author's launch and delegated choice 4A. This file is not canon, prose or a blind-reader packet. Current D-008 and trial requirements govern. No old candidate/rejected prose was read. `chapter-plan.json` contains the sequence; `writer-common.md` and the per-chapter packets are the bounded generation interface.

## Scope and shape

26 substantial chapters, target 4,700 whitespace-delimited Vietnamese prose units each, total target 122,200; acceptable working band 4,500–5,000, hard final minimum 4,000 per chapter and 110,000 total. Length comes from inhabited scenes, competing responsibilities and life beyond revelation, not recaps or a mechanical number of scares. Reveal begins at CH24 after about 108,100 units (88.5%); two chapters carry consequences. No new case is introduced.

The opening belongs to Lan Anh's ordinary visit to her mother, the home she must return to and the death she finds. Khanh enters in CH02 through family commerce. Thắng becomes the person with whom work is easier, waiting is bearable and fear can be admitted. The novel moves through a house trying to keep its people, a family trying to keep its story and small businesses trying to pay next week's costs. None is a disguise for an investigation.

## Calendar, physical space and final open-choice decisions

All dates are the characters' lunar dates in 2026: death on 13 tháng Mười; main aftermath reaches mùng 8 tháng Mười Một. No asserted Gregorian conversion, weekday or lunar-month length. Where elapsed arithmetic crosses the boundary, say `sang tháng` rather than count unsupported dates. Cool mixed weather, dry afternoons and a few damp nights; no permanent storm. Khanh arrived on 12, intending to resume Hanoi office work on 20. He actually spends 20–21 in Hanoi, returns the evening of 21, negotiates a bounded remote interval through the end of the month and resumes office life after the case. He never abandons engineering.

Chợ Bến and Xóm Trại are fictional within the existing northern setting. Market shop to Cường's gate is roughly 1.4 km by the main lane. Thắng's rented workshop is 800 m beyond the market; his family's two rented rooms are behind it, with a separate side entrance. Vân's packing premises are another 2 km out on a service road. Mai's produce shed is 300 m from the crossroads. Lan Anh's mother lives 7 km away along a normal paved road; a scooter trip is usually 20–25 minutes, not a second-perfect alibi. Thủy stays near her fictional Yên Phong garment workplace during work stretches; a journey home takes about an hour or more depending on her ride. None of these are claims about real travel times.

Cường's house: gate on the south/main lane; small paved front yard; a one-storey long house with front sitting room, middle passage and sleeping rooms off it; old rear storage/work room at the end, under retained timber roof members. The eastern covered side path leads from yard past kitchen to the back service door. Kitchen and washing place face a narrow side court; toilet is a separate rear outbuilding. Cường sleeps in the front-left bedroom; Toàn and Lan Anh in the right middle room. No upstairs, hidden staircase, secret tunnel or new escape door. Death and discovery occur in the rear storage room, entered from the middle passage or the known service door. The workbench, a tall shelf and an old roof cross-member explain the room's ordinary material use; do not turn them into a staging manual or retrospectively diagnosable evidence. Scuffle occurs in the front sitting room. Early visits establish the whole inhabited route through carrying chairs, washing bowls, fetching bedding and storing goods.

Cổn lives separately in the ancestral compound about 180 m away on the same branching lane. Its old brick side room and disused pond edge belong to the 1996 history. In 2026 that margin is a rough kitchen garden. The house where Cường dies is not where Thoa falls. Cổn is at his own house on night13; a married niece and her husband have come with food and stay into the evening. Their names need not become witness cards; nobody verifies every minute of his night.

Lan Anh is 29 (born1997), two school years ahead of Khanh; Toàn30; married without children at present. She sells small household goods on commission to acquaintances and wants a counter of her own near her mother's road. Her mother, bà Nguyệt (58), repairs and alters ordinary clothing at home, has sore eyes in the evenings and resents being treated as unable to decide her own life. No medical diagnosis or medical plot. Lan Anh's recurring reason for visits is helping sort sewing orders and bring meals; on night13 that visit was agreed the previous afternoon.

Thắng's wife is Nguyễn Thị Hòa,27; their daughter Bống is4. Hòa does paid clothing alterations at a machine in the front rented room and plans to rent a small covered counter sharing frontage with a hairdresser. She has saved36 million separately for deposit, machine repair and household reserve. She refuses to put it into Thắng's next rescue. Her clients, ambitions, parenting irritations and pleasure in competence remain real after his crime is known. She is neither accomplice nor last-minute source of all knowledge. Their marriage is not legally dissolved in this book; she moves with Bống to her elder sister's for the immediate aftermath and controls her own earnings.

Lê Đức Sơn,31, is Thắng's regular skilled helper, paid by agreed days/work stages, not a salaried employee whose national labour terms the narrator invents. His father needs help at a small feed stall; Sơn wants reliable pay to divide his time. He is owed12 million by the late work phase. He likes Thắng and resents covering for him. He leaves for another team after a handover, not as a sudden moral witness.

Trịnh Thị Vân,43, is the customer expanding a produce-sorting/packing premises. She knows Mai through ordinary buying and transport. She owes her own landlord rent before the new space earns money, dislikes wasted time more than grand revenge and wants usable doors/partitions, honest stock numbers and an affordable completion agreement. She appears before the reveal through actual work. She has no knowledge of night13 beyond what Cường said to her beforehand.

Cường's late wife died of an ordinary illness in2018, after Toàn became an adult; name not needed. No hidden murder. She was already pregnant before the1996 wedding; Toàn was born in autumn1996 (tháng Chín âm), making him30 at the present time. This is ordinary family chronology, not a new shame plot. Toàn's debt combines truck instalments and losses he conceals from gambling; no new loan-shark villain. The disputed pond margin matters to Thủy as decades of unpaid family obligation, not as Thắng's motive. No final legal allocation is decreed. Thủy demands that her support stop being treated as free and establishes a separate home/routine; a financial settlement is left for living negotiation.

## Objective night13 chronology — invisible as a checklist in prose

- Around15:00 Thủy confronts Cường over money and her father's demands. She deliberately frightens him with the idea that Thoa will not leave them in peace; no counterfeit ghost event or burglary. She leaves in time for an evening work shift beginning20:00 at the fictional workplace. Her co-worker and actual work scenes establish a lived shift, not a forensic alibi certificate.
- Around18:00–18:35 Thắng's first visit: asks for time. Cường refuses to postpone the customer meeting. Lan Anh encounters ordinary signs of the visit without hearing all its content.
- Around18:45 Lan Anh rides to bà Nguyệt; eats there, helps sort alterations, loses time to her mother's neighbour and an argument about taking too much family work home.
- Around19:40 Toàn comes home; asks for the land papers, quarrels, shoves Cường. Cường scratches him and tears his shirt. Toàn leaves about20:05. Cường is alive and mobile afterwards. No injury inference is needed.
- Around20:15 Cường speaks to Vân on the phone, confirming that she should bring the figures the next morning. She remembers the purpose, not an implausibly precise timestamp. This later relieves Toàn's fear of having physically killed his father, without making him blameless for violence or proving anything about suicide.
- Roughly20:35 Thắng returns by the ordinary gate/side path. He has already imagined killing Cường, while still hoping for a concession. No weapon-shopping sequence. The request fails; Cường intends to expose the transferred advance and demand his money. Thắng deliberately strangles Cường in the rear room and subsequently suspends the body to suggest suicide. The reveal states the causal act and choice, not procedural steps, duration, knot instruction or medical certainty. He leaves around21:10. These are internal anchors, not witness precision.
- Around21:30 Lan Anh returns, finds the hanging body, immediately shouts/calls neighbours and help. She does not diagnose, arrange evidence, wait for Toàn or first have a long private revelation. Help and necessary official handling are acknowledged in compressed ordinary terms; the novel neither depicts an investigation nor claims professionals were manipulated or formally certified suicide without basis. Family belief is its own category.
- Toàn returns on hearing the news, changes into another shirt. Lan Anh sees the torn one and later hears his account. She hides it in a tied laundry sack on14; chooses to discard it with ruined household cloth on17. It is not burned into recoverable fragments, retrieved intact or treated as forensic evidence.
- Funeral arrangements occupy14; service-supported burial on15 afternoon within about42 hours of discovery. No universal rite list or exact body-preservation claim. Particular small family practices remain attributed to that family. The body does not remain at home for the rest of the novel.

## Money and work, exact internal ledger

All sums are fictional agreements, not assertions of market rates. In early2026 Cường lent Thắng96 million for tools/independent work. Thắng repaid18; principal78 remains. Cường introduced Vân. Her agreed project is240 million, with120 advance,60 after the main frames are installed and60 after agreed completion. Of the first120, Thắng spent45 on this project's materials,65 filling an older job's loss and10 on accrued crew wages. Those120 are gone; the65 diversion is the concealed hinge. Another45 of supplier outlay,12 to Sơn and8 ancillary costs are needed to reach the next useful stage. This is a cash-flow trap, not the whole final profit/loss account. Hòa knows shortages exist but not the65 diversion or full78 debt. Her36 cannot solve all claims and she refuses to surrender it.

By night13 Cường plans to sit with Vân on14 and force the figures into the open. Killing does not magically cancel debt: Thắng imagines it will remove the one person who can confront him face-to-face and buy time through funeral disruption. He is partly right about delay, wrong about rescue and morally responsible. A murderer can continue to need food, finish a job, care for his daughter and do something brave. He never becomes a mastermind managing the whole book.

Early Thắng admits the second visit in a natural exchange about the last evening: he says he talked things through and left Cường alive. Khanh takes that to mean the pressing disagreement has eased. Later Thắng gives Hòa a specific claim of three more weeks; this is heard only after she starts taking responsibility for the inventory. Vân's actual meeting agreement, missing material and Sơn's unpaid work erode that claim. No one treats owing money, lying or future plans as proof of homicide. CH23 ends in a private unsettling exchange, then CH24 returns explicitly to night13 through Thắng's actual experience. Reader certainty arrives from truthful narration of the crime. Present-day admission then gives Khanh and Hòa sufficient moral knowledge without a detective speech. The authorities can be contacted at the end; investigation, custody and sentence remain outside the narrative and are not invented as accomplished facts.

## 1996 — separate human tragedy and sources

Thoa22 wants to use her left hand freely, draw little dress designs and try work at a small garment repair/cutting shop in town. She is not a genius saint or a secret wealthy heiress. She has petty irritations with Thủy, likes particular songs on her radio and wants an ordinary life that includes choosing her marriage. Family pressure seeks money/security for Cường's wedding. On the fatal summer day she packs a few clothes, quarrels, later returns for a personal thing; no prophecy, reliable dream or future ghost instruction.

Cường's violence takes place in the ancestral compound's old side room. A blow/shove causes her fall against a hard masonry edge; he makes a cursory check, leaves her without obtaining help and returns to his concerns. No assertion of exactly how long she could have survived. Thủy finds her dead that night; she did not witness the blow. Cổn learns from Cường what he did, orders concealment and controls the story. Cổn and Cường hide the body along the old pond margin; Thủy sees the wrapped removal toward that side, not the exact grave. The narrator's explicitly dated1996 chapter can show the causal truth without pretending a contemporary witness knows it all. The reader knows the fatal act before all living characters do.

Toàn knows only that Thoa died and was hidden: as an adult in2017 he overheard a family-record/naming dispute between his parents, during which Cường admitted that Thoa had died and the family concealed her death; his father then warned him not to repeat it. That conversation supplies the mother's partial knowledge too; she need not already know a murder secret. He never learned who struck her. He uses this partial knowledge to silence Lan Anh, believing scandal will ruin the whole household. This is cowardice with consequences, not knowledge of Thắng's crime. The radio remains continuously in Thủy's custody and has no role in the2017 conversation.

Thủy kept the broken radio among her own clothes through successive rooms. It enters as an ordinary repair request in CH12 after family pressure makes silence intolerable; no supernatural order, tape, automatic playback or evidentiary recording. Some old music associations belong to memory only; no unlicensed lyric blocks. Bà Lan remembers the young woman more warmly than the official runaway story; memory is bounded and imperfect. Bình remembers assisting an older outsider with a limited job near the pond; he did not see a body. Ông Hùng recalls his father refusing a coercive request with unclear purpose. Their fragments explain the persistence of fear, never solve the death.

CH17 gives Thoa a full day of lived1996 space and possibility. CH19–20 let Thủy name what she knows and Cổn lose the exclusive right to say what happened. His admission is partial, defensive and concerned with his own role. No automatic repentance. The family acknowledges Thoa by name and records her existence; proper next steps about remains are an unresolved human obligation, not a treasure-hunt climax. She is not confirmed to haunt in this experiment. That leaves Cường's real haunting distinct from the understandable living wish for Thoa's revenge.

## Hồn Cường: desire stronger than narrative memory

Internal consistency, not a cosmology to explain in prose. The continuing presence is genuinely Cường, not Thoa or a demon impersonator. It is anchored to the former household and close living relationships, especially Toàn and Lan Anh. It can briefly attend Thắng at the workshop because that dependency repeatedly structured Cường's life. It does not wander anywhere convenient, follow Khanh to Hanoi, access devices, read thoughts or hunt unrelated villagers. When Khanh witnesses it he is in another person's relation-space, not exercising a power.

The dead man retains bodily/social routines in disconnected islands: where to sit, who should bring rice, that a young son must stay close, that payment is owed, that he must have the last word. He may recognize a person in one context but address the wrong age or role in the next. He cannot maintain an ordinary back-and-forth or integrate present information into a continuous story. The same failure occurs over food, his wife's absence, his son's adult body and a repaired latch. It is not confined to the moment when naming a killer would be convenient. He never becomes lucid after discovery. No one can reliably summon or interview him.

Physical force exists at contact or close presence: obstructing a passage, holding a wrist/shoulder, displacing a seated person, exerting weight in a bed. It cannot levitate whole rooms, rewrite paperwork or transmit truth through object codes. Speech is short familiar address/demands, not a set of riddles. Significant scenes use body, space, social shame and violated intimacy; objects serve the encounter rather than perform an inventory of glitches. Daytime and company provide no guaranteed immunity, but incidents are not constant.

Developed frightening encounters, with room for writer invention at the local level:

1. CH01: after help has removed the body from the rear room, Lan Anh goes for bedding and meets the familiar figure waiting just inside the passage. He steps with her retreat instead of allowing her to pass; the living call her from the front while the dead is much closer. No voice explains this. She flees; other people do not acquire her whole perception.
2. CH03: in the crowded house, Toàn feels the paternal hand controlling the back of his neck as when he was a child. Looking across other sitting people, Khanh sees Cường low behind Toàn, face at the son's shoulder; the hand and unmoving familiarity are the horror, not a grotesque wound. Both struggle to react without terrifying everyone. Cường addresses the adult son as a little boy and never follows their answer.
3. CH07: an attempted family meal; the dead man takes his usual place and prevents Toàn rising, demanding food for the already-dead wife as though she is next door. Lan Anh moves the living away; a whole ordinary evening is lost. The wrong request shows lack of integrated memory without a lore lecture.
4. CH10: away from the house at her mother's, Lan Anh wakes to the weight and occupied shape of an adult man along the edge of the bed where she alone had lain. He tells her to get up for household work in the old tone; moving reveals too little space for the body that is there. Her mother sees enough of the doorway encounter to stop calling it fatigue. The ghost followed a close household person, not a cursed portable item.
5. CH15: in the workshop with real heavy glass to manage, Thắng and Sơn must keep their hands on an unstable load while Cường stands inside the space needed to set it down. He insists on an unrelated habitual money request, then addresses Khanh as someone else. Thắng helps prevent injury even while terrified. No magic glass shattering or implausible handling tutorial. A small ordinary cut is possible; no permanent disabling wound.
6. CH19: the family assembles to decide living arrangements. Cường's presence makes an occupied passage unusable, then he is against Toàn's back when they try the other known route. His clinging pressure is not an evil judgement on whoever confesses; vulnerable people suffer most. They leave together with assistance, not after solving a riddle or completing a rite.
7. CH25: after the human truth is exposed, Cường again expects the absent wife and the boy-son. The same incapacity remains. Lan Anh refuses to live there to satisfy that demand; Toàn helps her leave instead of commanding her to stay. No exorcistic declaration instantly works. He later uses the front part of the house by day, sleeping elsewhere.
8. CH26: an ordinary daylight return for useful household things ends with Cường occupying an interior space as if a family day will resume. The living can leave; the fact of his waiting is the residue. No new victim or new case hook.

Between these, dread can arise through avoidance, an interrupted task, someone refusing a bed, witnesses disagreeing about what they saw, and mundane consequences. Do not append a new sound/mirror/shadow to every chapter. Some ordinary scares are mundane, but the first substantive haunting is not retracted. No ritual specialist guarantees release. Human boundaries may reduce exposure and give people choices; they are not established supernatural cures.

## Fairness and recognition

Before CH24 there is no Thắng interior scene directly contemplating guilt; the narrator's chosen perspective is Khanh, Hòa, Sơn, Vân, Lan Anh, Thủy, Toàn or wider observed company. Avoid conspicuous refusal to describe him or repeated innocent praise. His warmth is allowed to exist without future qualification. His face need not repeatedly darken when a relevant word is spoken. Ordinary accounts can be incomplete because humans are occupied, but narrator fact cannot be false.

Recognition ladder: observed hanging → socially credible suicide account → haunting makes that account emotionally inadequate, not factually disproved → Thoa history explains family terror and concealed harm, not2026 death → daily unfinished work shows the purported final settlement did not happen → Khanh can no longer take Thắng's account for granted → late direct return confirms deliberate killing → present admission breaches friendship. The novel never argues `planned tomorrow therefore could not die by suicide`, `haunted therefore murdered`, `debt therefore killer` or `Thoa victim therefore revenant executioner`.

Khanh does not recruit witnesses, keep an investigation board, decode ghost messages or conduct a final reconstruction performance. He can ask why incompatible accounts differ when helping people do actual work. Hòa's right to know her own money and Vân's right to finish premises drive the late convergence. In CH23 Thắng asks Khanh for a private practical favour while the narrowing account becomes unbearable. CH24's first substantial pages return clearly to night13 and disclose murder; present admission can be brief and halting, not a monologue rehearsing every planted fact. Knowledge is sufficient for people to choose help/reporting, not for an invented court judgement.

## End states and quality risks to inspect after generation

Cổn lives, cared for but no longer controls every account or reunion. Thủy names Thoa, keeps the radio, changes her own obligations and does not acquire supernatural knowledge. Lan Anh temporarily stays with her mother and pursues her counter; Toàn admits his violence and stops demanding her false testimony. Their marriage has a possible future, not a performed reconciliation. Hòa protects Bống and earnings; no statement that either forgives. Thắng remains alive, admits responsibility in the narrative, agrees to face the next real-world step; the book does not depict a completed prosecution. Khanh accompanies the request for help rather than disposing of evidence or arranging escape. Cường persists; Thoa's dignity and unfinished material obligations persist. Khanh resumes his job, can still be frightened and can carry a useful bag for someone without needing to finish their story.

Inspect actual prose for: padding to meet length; too much uniform scene-ending dread; Hòa/Sơn/Vân turning into ledger voices; Cường's limits serving only suspense; Thoa occupying only testimony; over-signalling Thắng through fearful reactions; a neat legal/ritual ending; domestic work described only for clues. Preserve humour, warm friendship, selfish minor choices, skill and practical disagreement. Reader surprise remains a goal requiring a genuinely fresh reader, not something this already-informed architect can certify.

Checks of this design do not certify literary quality. Current sources were read live; memory was only an index pointing back to them. Design mutations are all local to the experiment. No unresolved fixed-canon conflict remains; omitted forensic and ritual specifics are intentional scope choices, not requirements to invent later.
