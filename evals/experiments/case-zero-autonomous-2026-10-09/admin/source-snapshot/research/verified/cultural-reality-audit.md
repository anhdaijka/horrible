# Cultural Reality Audit — Horrible

Status: verified research audit, not story canon.

Purpose: identify which real-world cultural/religious/ritual/commercial domains are sufficiently grounded for drafting and which still require research before a writer states concrete facts on-page.

## Bottom line

The project currently has a strong **research policy** but a weak **verified fact corpus**.

Discovery 08 and Discovery 11 correctly warn against collapsing Buddhism, Đạo Mẫu, ancestor practice, folk ritual, funerals, taboo, folklore and votive-paper commerce into one generic occult system. However, those files mostly define boundaries and research requirements. They do not yet provide enough scene-level verified detail for a writer to improvise confidently.

Until the relevant domain has a verified note, the writer must not invent a real Vietnamese practice merely because it makes the plot convenient.

## Priority matrix

### P0 — required before Chapter 2–3 / Case Zero detail

#### 1. Votive-paper retail, wholesale and custom-order reality

Current state: **partially researched, verified starter note exists**.

Need to know:
- what products a mixed retail shop can plausibly keep as ordinary stock;
- which products are commonly wholesale/imported versus custom-made or locally finished;
- how customers actually ask for goods and how shopkeepers distinguish purpose/use;
- terminology such as `hàng mã`, `vàng mã`, `đồ mã`, `mã thờ`, `mã tiến`, `hóa`, and regional/shop-specific shorthand;
- seasonal demand patterns around Tết, rằm, giỗ, funerals and other occasions;
- what kinds of personalization are plausible and what would require a specialist craftsperson;
- realistic packaging, storage, fire risk, order-taking and delivery practices;
- what the family shop knows from customers versus what belongs to ritual specialists.

Important boundary: the Khanh family business model — retail + custom orders + limited finishing, with wholesale sourcing — is a **fictional family-specific model chosen for the story**. It must not be generalized into a universal claim about Northern Vietnamese votive-paper shops.

#### 2. Funeral / immediate post-death workflow in contemporary Northern Vietnam

Current state: **insufficient for scene-level writing**.

Need a verified, locality-aware map for:
- hospital/home death and practical family decisions;
- báo tang / obituary / funeral-service logistics;
- khâm liệm / nhập quan terminology and whether/how it is used in the chosen family context;
- burial versus cremation choices in a contemporary semi-urbanizing Northern family;
- mourning roles, visitors, food, money/envelopes, wake timing and procession;
- where Buddhist rites, family rites and local custom overlap or diverge;
- which parts vary strongly by family, locality, generation and religious affiliation.

Do not create one universal “Northern funeral sequence”.

#### 3. Funeral and memorial use of votive goods

Current state: **insufficient**.

Need to verify:
- which goods are plausible for funeral versus 49-day / 100-day / giỗ / household ancestor offerings;
- what is burned, displayed, offered or avoided in different settings;
- which practices are folk/family customs rather than Buddhist doctrine;
- whether a requested item/order would sound ordinary, unusual, extravagant or incorrect to Lan.

This matters directly to Case Zero because the horror depends on the order being ordinary enough to be believable yet specific enough to become disturbing.

### P1 — required before recurring religious/practitioner scenes

#### 4. Buddhism versus folk ancestor practice

Current state: **high-level boundary verified; scene detail incomplete**.

Already safe at high level:
- burning votive paper is not a Buddhist doctrinal requirement;
- the Vietnam Buddhist Sangha has urged Buddhist establishments to discourage/remove votive-paper burning;
- 49-day practice exists in Vietnamese Buddhist contexts, but Buddhist traditions and household customs do not all describe death/rebirth or memorial timing identically.

Still need:
- how a Northern Vietnamese monk/nun would naturally speak about death, cầu siêu, hồi hướng, 49 days and ancestor memorials without turning into a theology lecture;
- distinction between what a family does “ở chùa” and what it does at home;
- temple etiquette and ordinary layperson behavior for the exact site used in a scene.

Hard rule: do not put folk claims about sending paper goods to the dead into a monk’s mouth as “Buddhist teaching” unless the scene is explicitly showing a mistaken or contested claim.

#### 5. Đạo Mẫu / Thờ Mẫu lived practice

Current state: **high-level UNESCO/academic grounding exists; operational detail incomplete**.

Already safe at high level:
- this is a living Vietnamese belief tradition with ceremonies, festivals, music, costume and spirit-medium practice;
- hầu đồng should not be treated as generic demonic possession;
- practitioners include multiple roles rather than one generic `thầy`;
- commercialization is a real safeguarding concern but does not define the tradition.

Still need before Thủy or a full ritual scene:
- exact role vocabulary relevant to her (`thanh đồng`, `đồng thầy`, `thủ nhang`, `cung văn`, assistants, etc.);
- structure and etiquette of a hầu đồng session at the level needed on-page;
- relationship between bản hội, temple/palace space, musicians, offerings, gifts/lộc and money;
- which objects, colors, songs, gestures and divine figures belong to which context;
- what is public, private, negotiable or sensitive.

Do not use a generic invented “medium ceremony” and label it Đạo Mẫu.

#### 6. Ritual-specialist ecology

Current state: **insufficient**.

Need to distinguish by locality/tradition:
- thầy cúng;
- thầy bói / divination practitioners;
- folk ritual specialists;
- practitioners connected to Đạo Mẫu;
- Buddhist clergy;
- funeral-service ritual personnel;
- family elders with inherited household knowledge.

The grandfather’s exact historical social role remains deliberately ambiguous in canon, but when the novel finally shows what he physically did, the actions and vocabulary must come from a researched practice rather than a fantasy “exorcist” composite.

### P1 — taboo / supernatural-folklore risk

#### 7. Kiêng kị / taboo

Current state: **conceptual caution good; verified atlas poor**.

Important research finding: taboos can be highly local, deity-specific, family-specific and historically variable. Research on Thành hoàng worship in the Northern Delta explicitly emphasizes both shared and village-specific taboo forms.

Therefore do **not** create a universal Northern taboo checklist such as “never do X after a death” unless the claim is well sourced for the relevant context.

For each taboo used in plot, record:
- who believes it;
- locality/community;
- earliest/strongest source;
- whether it is historical, current, declining, contested or internet-amplified;
- whether breaking it has a social consequence, supernatural interpretation, or author-confirmed effect.

High-risk topics requiring their own notes before main-plot use:
- trùng tang / Thần Trùng;
- death away from home / death on road;
- giờ xấu / tuổi kỵ in funeral handling;
- grave and burial taboos;
- cải táng / bốc mộ;
- calling the dead, dream instructions, spirit-entry claims;
- corpse-related folklore such as `quỷ nhập tràng`.

#### 8. Folklore provenance

Current state: **Discovery 11 is a good candidate map, not a verified atlas**.

Terms like `ma trơi`, `chó đội nón mê`, `ma xó`, `ma giữ của`, `quỷ nhập tràng`, `trùng tang`, `oan hồn`, `cô hồn`, `vong nhập`, etc. still need source-by-source provenance and regional notes before narrator-level cultural claims.

Internet ghost stories may be used as modern testimony, but must not be silently upgraded into ancient Vietnamese tradition.

### P2 — needed before deeper Volume 1 cultural scenes

#### 9. Ancestor altar and household ritual material culture

Current state: **insufficient**.

Need:
- ordinary altar layouts and household variation;
- bát hương / ảnh thờ / bài vị usage by family context;
- rằm, mùng một, giỗ, Tết and other household rhythms;
- who usually prepares what in a family like Khanh’s;
- what changes after a death and when integration into ancestor worship may occur;
- differences between religious doctrine and family custom.

Avoid treating one family’s altar arrangement as a national rule.

#### 10. Death anniversaries, 49 days, 100 days, mãn tang

Current state: **partial sources; not enough for canonical sequence**.

Research already shows that 49-day and 100-day observances exist in Vietnamese practice, while Buddhist interpretations and local/family customs differ. Some families prioritize 49 days, some 100 days, and ritual meaning is not uniform.

Need case-specific decision before any family is shown performing them.

#### 11. Burial, cremation and cải táng in contemporary Northern Vietnam

Current state: **good academic leads; no story-specific decision**.

Recent scholarship documents movement from older burial/cát táng patterns toward cremation and one-time burial in parts of the Northern Delta. Scholarship also cautions against treating cải táng as an eternal, uniform Vietnamese tradition.

This is valuable for Horrible because modernization itself can create believable family conflict without a ghost.

#### 12. Sacred-site distinctions and etiquette

Current state: **high-level rule only**.

Need verified practical distinctions for any site shown closely:
- chùa;
- đình;
- đền;
- phủ;
- miếu;
- private điện / household worship space where relevant.

For each used site, research what is worshipped there, who manages it, what visitors ordinarily do, what offerings/practices are appropriate, and which actions are discouraged or prohibited.

### P2 — commerce, law and modernity

#### 13. Regulation, fire safety and public policy around votive burning

Current state: **basic legal/policy grounding exists**.

Useful verified points:
- Vietnamese cultural regulations have restricted burning ritual paper goods within festival areas;
- state cultural agencies and Buddhist authorities have repeatedly encouraged reduction of votive burning in religious/festival spaces;
- current practice is therefore not simply “everyone burns everywhere”. Site rules, designated burning areas and fire-safety concerns matter.

Need scene-specific verification if police, local government, temple management or legal penalties become plot-relevant.

#### 14. Economics of belief and ritual

Current state: **academic leads, insufficient scene detail**.

Need:
- how money moves in ordinary temple/palace/festival contexts;
- offerings, donations, service fees and informal reciprocity;
- how commercialization debates are described by practitioners themselves;
- price/time costs of specialized ritual goods without turning belief into a caricature of greed.

#### 15. Shop language and customer voice

Current state: **major prose risk; insufficient research**.

A believable shop needs more than a product list. Research should collect natural language for:
- customers asking for ordinary goods;
- customers describing the deceased / recipient / purpose;
- ordering by package, piece, set, size or ritual use;
- seller clarification questions;
- bargaining, substitutions, unavailable stock and pickup/delivery;
- what details Lan would recognize as normal versus strange.

This is one of the highest-value future research tasks because dialogue authenticity and cultural accuracy meet here.

## Current evidence base: what is actually verified enough now

### Đạo Mẫu / Mother Goddess practices — HIGH confidence at high level

UNESCO recognizes Practices related to the Viet beliefs in the Mother Goddesses of Three Realms as living intangible heritage. The documentation describes worship, ceremonies, festivals, spirit-medium ritual, traditional music/costume/dance, and multiple practitioner roles. UNESCO also identifies over-commercialization as a safeguarding concern.

Sources:
- https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/practices-related-to-the-viet-beliefs-in-the-mother-goddesses-of-three-realms-01064
- https://ich.unesco.org/en/decisions/11.COM/10.B.37

### Buddhist boundary on votive burning — HIGH confidence at high level

The Vietnam Buddhist Sangha has issued guidance asking Buddhist establishments to discourage/remove votive-paper burning; government cultural reporting also references that guidance. Therefore `burning votive paper = Buddhist doctrine` is unsafe and generally wrong.

Sources:
- https://baochinhphu.vn/thuc-hien-nep-song-van-minh-tai-cac-le-hoi-co-so-tho-tu-phat-giao-102252280.htm
- https://vhttdl.daklak.gov.vn/Articles/Detail/2747
- https://phatgiao.org.vn/cung-co-hon-co-nen-dot-vang-ma-d100216.html

### Festival-area burning / public-management boundary — HIGH confidence

Cultural regulation has explicitly prohibited burning `đồ mã` in festival areas, and current cultural-management guidance continues to regulate incense/votive burning by site and designated practice rather than treating it as unrestricted.

Sources:
- https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?classid=1&docid=99404&orggroupid=4&pageid=27160
- https://bvhttdl.gov.vn/Pages/chi-tiet.aspx?url=%2Fkhuyen-nghi-khong-dot-do-ma-vang-ma-trong-cac-co-so-tho-tu-di-tich-va-le-hoichuyen-bien-tu-nhan-thuc-den-hanh-dong-20191024091349218.htm

### Votive-paper craft and supply — MEDIUM-HIGH confidence for broad facts

Recent reporting documents specialized craft traditions, wholesale circulation, mass-produced versus specialist goods, custom craftsmanship, modern modeled objects, and product differentiation. Song Hồ / Đông Hồ remains associated with votive production and supply, while other production centers specialize differently.

Sources:
- https://nhandan.vn/bac-vang-hang-ma-ky-1-post906571.html
- https://kinhtedothi.vn/thang-co-hon-o-ngoi-lang-san-xuat-do-coi-am-lon-nhat-ha-noi
- https://baobacninh.vn/vai-net-ve-tranh-dan-gian-ong-ho-45210.html

### Funeral / burial variation — MEDIUM-HIGH confidence for the rule “do not universalize”

Academic work documents differences between Buddhist, Catholic and customary funerary practice, changing burial patterns in the Northern Delta, and the historically contingent nature of cải táng. This supports the project rule that a family/locality must be specified before detailed ritual claims.

Sources:
- https://vjol.info.vn/rsr/en/article/view/3261/
- https://vjol.info.vn/tapchiviendantochoc/vi/article/view/110579/
- https://vjol.info.vn/tapchiviendantochoc/vi/article/view/110580/

### Locality of taboo — MEDIUM-HIGH confidence

Recent religious-studies work on Thành hoàng worship in the Northern Delta explicitly treats taboo as containing both broad patterns and village/local deity-specific variation. This supports avoiding a universal “Vietnamese taboo” list.

Source:
- https://vjol.info.vn/rsr/en/article/view/126439/

## Writer gate

Before writing a concrete real-world practice, ask:

1. Is this a researched fact, a family-specific fictional choice, or a character belief?
2. If researched, where is the verified note/source?
3. If not researched, can the scene avoid the detail until later?
4. If the detail must exist, can it be explicitly fictional/local without pretending to document a real tradition?
5. Does the wording accidentally turn a contested belief into narrator-certified truth?

When uncertain, prefer less detail over plausible-sounding invention.
