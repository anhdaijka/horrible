# Verified Research — Votive-Paper Commerce v1

Status: verified starter note, not story canon.

Scope: enough broad grounding to stop a writer from inventing the shop from scratch. This is **not** yet a complete ethnography of a contemporary Northern Vietnamese votive-paper retailer.

## What can be treated as broadly supported

### 1. `Hàng mã` is not one uniform product class

Modern and traditional paper effigies/products can serve different functions and vary substantially by region, maker, ritual context and customer. Recent reporting on specialist craft distinguishes forms used for worship/display from forms intended to be burned after rites, and notes that mass-market and specialist/custom products coexist.

Source:
- Nhân Dân, `Bạc vàng hàng mã (kỳ 1)`, 2025: https://nhandan.vn/bac-vang-hang-ma-ky-1-post906571.html

Confidence: medium-high.

Do not infer:
- that every shop or practitioner uses the same terminology;
- that `mã thờ` / `mã tiến` are the only categories;
- that every customer knows specialist terminology.

### 2. Wholesale supply and specialist production centers are plausible

Recent reporting describes urban sellers importing goods from surrounding production centers and identifies local specializations. Song Hồ / Đông Hồ in Bắc Ninh remains associated with votive production; other places such as Phúc Am / Văn Hội have their own production histories/specializations.

Sources:
- Nhân Dân, 2025: https://nhandan.vn/bac-vang-hang-ma-ky-1-post906571.html
- Kinh tế & Đô thị, 2023: https://kinhtedothi.vn/thang-co-hon-o-ngoi-lang-san-xuat-do-coi-am-lon-nhat-ha-noi
- Báo Bắc Ninh, 2019: https://baobacninh.vn/vai-net-ve-tranh-dan-gian-ong-ho-45210.html

Confidence: medium-high for broad supply-network plausibility.

Story implication:
- it is plausible that Khanh's family sells stock made elsewhere rather than manufacturing everything;
- sourcing some goods from a Song Hồ / Đông Hồ network is plausible;
- the exact supplier, route and commercial relationship should remain family-specific fiction unless separately verified.

### 3. Custom and specialist orders exist

Specialist craftspeople historically and currently can make highly customized items, with product design depending on customer request, scale, materials and context. Recent reporting explicitly describes customers commissioning non-standard forms and craftspeople adapting structures and details.

Source:
- Nhân Dân, 2025: https://nhandan.vn/bac-vang-hang-ma-ky-1-post906571.html

Confidence: high for existence of custom work; low for any specific item until researched.

Story implication:
- a `custom order` at Khanh's shop is plausible;
- the family may plausibly do limited finishing or coordinate with a specialist maker;
- do not assume the family can fabricate any arbitrary ritual object overnight.

### 4. Common paper-goods imagery changes with contemporary consumer life

Reporting documents modern modeled goods such as phones, motorbikes, houses and other consumer objects alongside older forms.

Sources:
- Nhân Dân, 2025: https://nhandan.vn/bac-vang-hang-ma-ky-1-post906571.html
- Kinh tế & Đô thị, 2018/2023: https://kinhtedothi.vn/lang-nghe-phuc-am-hoi-ha-don-thang-vu-lan-323041 ; https://kinhtedothi.vn/thang-co-hon-o-ngoi-lang-san-xuat-do-coi-am-lon-nhat-ha-noi

Confidence: high for existence; low for prevalence in a specific household/ritual.

Do not infer:
- that extravagant modern goods are normal for every funeral or giỗ;
- that `trần sao âm vậy` is a universally accepted doctrine rather than a widespread folk explanation.

### 5. Seasonality exists but should not become a fixed universal calendar

Media reporting repeatedly describes increased production/sales around major ritual seasons such as Tết and Rằm tháng Bảy. Ordinary rằm, mùng một, giỗ and other family occasions also appear in discussions of votive use, but exact demand and product mix vary.

Sources:
- Kinh tế & Đô thị, 2023: https://kinhtedothi.vn/thang-co-hon-o-ngoi-lang-san-xuat-do-coi-am-lon-nhat-ha-noi
- Nhân Dân, 2010: https://nhandan.vn/dot-vang-ma-trong-le-tet-truyen-thong-post570940.html

Confidence: high for seasonal variation; medium/low for exact product mix.

### 6. Burning is socially and institutionally contested

Contemporary Vietnam includes state cultural guidance, Buddhist institutional discouragement and environmental/fire-safety concerns around votive burning. A modern shop therefore exists inside a changing social practice rather than a static timeless tradition.

Sources:
- Government cultural regulation: https://vanban.chinhphu.vn/?classid=1&docid=99404&orggroupid=4&pageid=27160
- Government/Buddhist-establishment reporting: https://baochinhphu.vn/thuc-hien-nep-song-van-minh-tai-cac-le-hoi-co-so-tho-tu-phat-giao-102252280.htm
- Ministry of Culture reporting: https://bvhttdl.gov.vn/Pages/chi-tiet.aspx?url=%2Fkhuyen-nghi-khong-dot-do-ma-vang-ma-trong-cac-co-so-tho-tu-di-tich-va-le-hoichuyen-bien-tu-nhan-thuc-den-hanh-dong-20191024091349218.htm

Confidence: high.

Story implication:
- customers may differ generationally in how much they buy/burn;
- some religious sites may discourage or regulate burning;
- Lan can plausibly treat this as commerce shaped by changing customer habits rather than a sacred timeless rule.

## Family-shop model: fiction versus evidence

### Supported plausibility

It is plausible for a contemporary shop to:
- carry mass-produced stock;
- source from wholesalers / production villages;
- receive unusual customer requests;
- coordinate with specialist makers;
- sell a mixture of low-cost ordinary paper goods and more elaborate products.

### Fictional choices specific to Khanh's family

The following remain story design, not claims about all real shops:
- Lan is the primary operator;
- the shop keeps a specific mix of stock;
- the family does `limited finishing/customization` in-house;
- exact supplier(s) are connected to Song Hồ / Đông Hồ;
- exact pickup/delivery rhythm;
- exact customer ledger format;
- exact markup, payment habits and credit relationships.

These choices are allowed, but the writer must not narrate them as regional universals.

## Still unverified before Case Zero order scene

Research before finalizing Chapter 2–3 order dialogue:

1. What would Lan naturally call the categories/items in her own shop?
2. What unit language does a customer use — `bộ`, `tập`, `xấp`, `mâm`, `lễ`, item names, or context-specific combinations?
3. Which details are normally needed for a personalized funeral/memorial order?
4. Is writing the recipient's full name / age / address on an order, item or accompanying paper normal for any comparable practice, or should this be a family-shop fictional administrative habit?
5. What specific ordinary goods would plausibly form the Case Zero package?
6. Which item would be unusual enough for Lan to remember without making the order obviously supernatural?
7. What can a small shop modify itself (size, label, packaging, simple clothing details) versus what requires a specialist craftsperson?
8. Realistic lead time and pickup/payment behavior for a custom piece such as an old-style radio model.

Until these are answered, do **not** lock the final Case Zero product list or customer wording.
