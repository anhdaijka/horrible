# Opening Architecture v2 — Horror-First Reset

Status: **new working architecture after author-approved full reset**. Not manuscript canon. No prose from the rejected chapters should be reused by default.

## Objective

The new opening must accomplish three things at once:

1. **promise a ghost story immediately;**
2. introduce Khanh / family / votive shop / market through an active story rather than a long preliminary slice-of-life block;
3. preserve Case Zero's information order so the uncanny grows by recontextualization rather than narrator explanation.

## Recommended opening mechanism

Use a **controlled retrospective hook**.

The story may show the reader a premise-level consequence before rewinding to the order itself.

This is not treated as a spoiler because `the order existed before the man's death` is the Case Zero hook, not its solution. The unanswered questions remain:

- Who was the woman?
- Why did she order it?
- How could she know?
- Did she know?
- Why that cassette radio?
- Why does she later resemble somebody else?
- What does the buried family history have to do with any of this?

The retrospective frame supplies storyteller confidence and immediate genre promise without forcing characters in the earlier order scene to know future information.

# Opening sequence

## Chapter 1 — working function: `The name was already there`

Working title: **unlocked**. Candidate only: `Bốn ngày trước`.

### Opening image / hook

Open **after Đỗ Văn Quang's unexpected death**, during the practical beginning of funeral preparation.

A member of his household comes to bà Lan's shop for ordinary funeral/votive goods and identifies the deceased by name.

Behind / beside the normal transaction is a custom order that has not been collected — ideally physically set aside in the shop with a simple tag or ledger reference bearing the same recipient name.

The name catches bà Lan or Khanh because it is already present in the shop workflow.

They check the order date.

The order predates the death.

Do **not** explain the full mystery yet.

This opening should produce the reader question:

> **Vì sao đồ mã cho người đàn ông này đã được đặt từ trước khi ông ấy chết?**

### Retrospective turn

After the premise lands, rewind to **four days earlier** (or the exact locked interval once timeline is finalized).

Now introduce:

- Khanh recently home from Japan;
- bà Lan and ông Hùng through active shop work;
- the shop/market as lived social space;
- the mysterious purchaser.

The reader knows something bad will happen to the named man. The characters do not.

This creates **dramatic irony instead of narrator leakage**.

### Order scene

At the time of purchase:

- the woman looks ordinary;
- the shop assumes the requested goods concern a deceased recipient, because that is the natural default for the goods/context;
- nobody asks whether the man is alive;
- nobody discusses severe illness or advance preparation;
- Khanh records the transaction because he happens to be helping;
- the custom cassette radio is a practical production problem;
- `người đặt` and recipient identity are recorded as ordinary administrative detail;
- left-hand behavior appears naturally;
- the three-day pickup request is practical, not mystical.

The woman leaves.

### Chapter-end image

End on a concrete transaction residue rather than an explanatory thought.

Preferred image:

- the named custom order / bundle / radio reference is labeled and placed on the shop shelf to wait for pickup.

Because the reader already knows the man will die, the ordinary label now carries dread without ominous narrator explanation.

### Chapter 1 genre reward

The reward is not a jumpscare.

It is the impossible-looking chronological fact established at the opening:

**the order existed before the death.**

The rest of the chapter earns trust by showing how ordinary the original transaction really was.

## Chapter 2 — working function: `The ordinary explanation breaks`

### Time movement

Return to the present line after the rewind.

If the purchaser was supposed to collect the order on day three and did not appear, this can be mentioned briefly as normal shop inconvenience — do not make the no-show supernatural by itself.

On day four, ông Quang dies unexpectedly after the nighttime stair fall.

### Family / shop encounter

His family comes for funeral goods or otherwise supplies a natural route for bà Lan/Khanh to connect the death with the order.

Now, for the **first time**, characters consciously understand:

- the order was made while ông Quang was still alive.

Only now should the real-life ordinary hypothesis appear:

> Có thể nhà biết ông ấy bệnh nặng, sợ không qua khỏi nên có người mua chuẩn bị trước.

This is no longer leakage because the anomaly has now been discovered.

### Immediate pressure against that hypothesis

Do not leave the hypothesis intact for too long.

Through natural social information — family reaction, neighborhood knowledge, recent sighting, routine activity — establish that:

- ông Quang had appeared healthy / normally active;
- the household was not expecting his death;
- the stair fall was a shock rather than the end of an obvious terminal decline.

This does not scientifically prove perfect health. It destroys the **expected-death preparation** explanation at the social level.

### Chapter-end escalation

The earlier order should now feel much worse than it did on first reading.

End with one hard piece of pressure, e.g.:

- nobody in the household knows who placed that order;
- nobody recognizes the purchaser name in the obvious present-day family context;
- or bà Lan realizes the woman was unusually certain about the exact old radio.

Do not unload all Case Zero clues at once.

---

## Chapter 3 — working function: `The woman becomes the mystery`

Only after the chronology is disturbing should attention shift from **the order** to **the purchaser**.

Possible sequence:

1. check the original ledger / order slip;
2. verify no simple namesake or obvious family preparation explanation;
3. recover CCTV for an ordinary shop reason;
4. discover resemblance to the surviving sister;
5. sister denies visiting;
6. handedness mismatch becomes supportive evidence rather than a magic clue;
7. purchaser name begins opening the older family layer.

The chapter should still contain horror pressure, not become a procedural checklist.

Each investigative fact should make the original ordinary woman harder to explain.

# Character introduction strategy

Do not run a separate `meet the family` chapter.

Introduce people when the active story needs them.

### Khanh

Establish:

- recently back from Japan;
- software-engineer life waiting in Hanoi;
- familiar but slightly displaced at home;
- capable of ordinary fear and curiosity;
- analytical only when a contradiction becomes real.

No need to explain all of this before the order hook.

### Bà Lan

Introduce through competence at the shop and her reaction to a name/date she knows should be administratively boring.

Her baseline matters because later a small interruption in that baseline can carry fear.

### Ông Hùng

Bring him in through whatever practical job intersects the active scene. No obligation to include him in every opening beat.

### Mai

Do **not** insert Mai merely because she is opening cast.

Bring her in when her actual life/logistics naturally crosses the case or homecoming. Strictly platonic lock remains.

# Horror texture rules for the new opening

## Allowed

- narrator foreshadowing;
- retrospective framing;
- anticipation before characters are afraid;
- a memorable image linked to the order/death chronology;
- deliberate dread;
- quiet supernatural implication;
- ordinary shop detail becoming sinister after context changes.

## Avoid

- narrator telling the reader `nothing seemed strange`;
- characters discussing `buying in advance for living people` during the original order;
- a fake scare with a mundane punchline merely to demonstrate skepticism;
- several pages of market/family routine before the story question begins;
- turning every line about the purchaser into horror coding;
- dumping all fair-play clues in the first discovery scene.

---

# Why this opening is different from the discarded prototype

Old model:

`ordinary life → ordinary life → fake scare → ordinary explanation → order → narrator explains why order is normal`

New model:

`uncanny chronological hook → rewind → ordinary sale charged by dramatic irony → death → natural hypothesis → hypothesis breaks → purchaser becomes frightening`

The new structure allows the shop and family to feel ordinary **inside a story that is already moving**.
