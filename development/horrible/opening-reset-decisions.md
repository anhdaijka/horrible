# Opening Reset Decisions

Status: author-approved reset guard.

## What remains accepted

- Core protagonist / family / setting concept.
- Family votive-paper shop beside the market crossroads.
- Khanh recently returned from Japan and still works as a software engineer.
- Case Zero core premise: a woman places an order associated with a man; later chronology reveals the order predates his unexpected death.
- Advance purchase for a living person can be completely normal when relatives expect a death; the disturbing part is later evidence that this victim was not expected to die.
- Custom old-style cassette radio remains a promising Case Zero clue unless later redesign replaces it.
- Left-handed purchaser behavior remains a possible fair-play clue unless later redesign replaces it.
- Purchaser identity may eventually connect to a long-dead sibling / buried family history.
- Cultural research and author domain knowledge remain binding.

## What is unlocked again

Anything invented only by the rejected Chapter 1–2 prototypes is **not automatically carried forward**, including:

- working purchaser name `Đỗ Thị Hường`;
- working victim name `Đỗ Văn Quang`;
- `xóm Đình Ngoài` and the old-school / well address shorthand;
- exact shop ledger wording used in the prototype;
- exact physical appearance/clothing of the purchaser;
- exact ordinary product list;
- exact dialogue;
- exact scene order from either rejected chapter;
- exact first-appearance descriptions from the old prose;
- exact chapter titles `Tiếng chợ` / `Người đặt`.

These may coincidentally be re-selected later, but only through a fresh deliberate decision.

## Revelation-order lock

The shop does **not** consciously treat the original transaction as `buying in advance for a living person` while the purchaser is standing there.

The intended sequence is:

1. ordinary order, recipient naturally assumed to be deceased;
2. later unexpected death of the named recipient;
3. discovery that the order date predates the death;
4. only now: `perhaps relatives knew he was seriously ill and prepared early`;
5. later social evidence shows he was outwardly healthy / household did not expect death;
6. the purchaser becomes increasingly difficult to explain.

This revelation order supersedes the rejected Chapter 2 prototype.

## Genre reset lock

- Chapter 1 must deliver a ghost-story / uncanny promise.
- No fake first scare whose purpose is to prove a mundane explanation.
- No requirement to hide supernatural possibility for realism.
- Confirmed supernatural events are allowed elsewhere in the series.
- Case Zero itself may remain ambiguous if that is the best artistic result; this does not define the metaphysical policy of the entire series.
