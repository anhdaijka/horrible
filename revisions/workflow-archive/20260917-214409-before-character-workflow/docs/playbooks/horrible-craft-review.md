# Horrible Creative–Safety Review Playbook

The workflow protects two things that must not be confused: literary life and story truth. A single all-purpose checklist made agents write toward compliance, then review their own intentions. The active process separates those jobs.

## 1. Authority

The author's newest direct instruction remains final. Canon, chronology, knowledge, reveal locks, durable state, and verified real-world claims remain hard boundaries. No draft, review, or revision becomes canon without explicit author acceptance.

Creative freedom inside those boundaries is real. A writer may discover dialogue, silence, rhythm, scene micro-order, gesture, incidental action, sensory emphasis, and non-durable detail. The repository does not prescribe a house rhythm, a gesture vocabulary, a quota of clues or senses, or a mandatory chapter length.

## 2. Context firewall

| Role | Receives | Must not receive by default |
|---|---|---|
| Preflight | Relevant authority sources | Rejected prose as a model |
| Writer | Approved `writing-brief.md`; immediate accepted preceding prose only when needed | Internal preflight, full directives/style bible/matrix, reviews, old drafts |
| Literary reviewer | Candidate prose | Brief, preflight, writer rationale, safety sources, previous review |
| Safety reviewer | Candidate, internal preflight, smallest sufficient authority set | Literary preferences or technique quotas |
| Reviser | Candidate plus both reviews | Unbounded repository context unless a cited safety issue requires it |

If context leakage cannot be avoided, disclose it. A literary reviewer who helped write or revise the candidate marks `CONTEXT_INDEPENDENCE: COMPROMISED` and cannot issue the final fresh-context PASS.

## 3. Preflight as projection, not prose specification

Preflight does the heavy authority work once. It produces:

- an internal safety card containing chronology, knowledge/reveal locks, durable state, factual dependencies, exclusions, and approval;
- a short writing brief containing only dramatic charge, five to eight hard facts, immediate character wants, unsaid material, the end-state shift, and at most one optional concrete charge.

The writer brief must leave execution unsolved. If it dictates line order, gestures, sensory inventory, dialogue samples, or how every clue should feel, it has failed.

## 4. Drafting

The writer writes prose, not a compliance demonstration. The central test is whether people are trying to do something to one another under pressure. Information should enter because someone wants, avoids, controls, tests, wounds, protects, or misreads—not because the outline needs a fact delivered.

`draft.md` contains manuscript prose only. Audit notes, open issues, claim deltas, and self-explanations live outside it.

## 5. Blind literary review

The literary reviewer reads the entire candidate before learning its intended function. The review asks:

- Does the scene have desire, resistance, and change?
- Do voices arise from relationship, status, history, and immediate tactics?
- Does dialogue sound arranged for the reader?
- Do gestures reveal something, or merely subtitle an emotion already stated?
- Does narration trust the reader or pre-interpret every beat?
- Do images and horror accumulate meaning and leave an after-effect?
- Does rhythm feel chosen, or generated from a repeated AI pattern?
- Does the chapter feel like a story, or a successful execution log?

There is no finding quota, mandatory span count, word-count band, sensory inventory, or required horror device. Quote only enough evidence to make the diagnosis falsifiable. A systemic failure may require a substantial rewrite; “smallest edit” is not a literary virtue when the scene's engine is wrong.

Verdicts:

- `PASS`: publishable at the current literary standard, subject to safety and author approval.
- `REVISE_REQUIRED`: page-level or structural literary problems remain.
- `PROVISIONAL`: useful diagnosis, but context independence is compromised.
- `INCOMPLETE`: the candidate could not be fully read or assessed.

## 6. Safety review

The safety reviewer checks only:

- canon and immutable outcome;
- chronology and location;
- character knowledge, perception, inference, and reveal order;
- clue source, custody, and conclusion limits;
- durable character, object, relationship, and supernatural state;
- real cultural, legal, medical, forensic, trade, or institutional claims;
- rejected or superseded material;
- new material choices that require the author.

Every blocking finding cites both the candidate location and authority source. For an unverified real-world claim, the allowed repairs are research, omission, explicit local/family invention, or attribution to a character's belief.

The safety reviewer does not judge beauty, dread, dialogue, pacing, imagery, physicality, or word count. `SAFETY_REVIEW: PASS` means only that no blocking boundary violation was found.

## 7. Revision

Revision uses two scales:

- restore hard safety boundaries with the smallest effective correction;
- repair literary failure at its real scale, including rebuilding an exchange or scene when necessary.

The reviser protects successful living material instead of normalizing every sentence. Clean prose goes to `revised.md`; finding dispositions go to `revision-audit.md`. Any meaningful revision receives new literary and safety reviews.

## 8. Canonization gate

Canonization requires all of the following for the same candidate:

1. fresh-context `LITERARY_REVIEW: PASS`;
2. `SAFETY_REVIEW: PASS`;
3. explicit `AUTHOR_ACCEPTANCE: APPROVED`;
4. a canon diff containing durable state only;
5. successful continuity/link validation.

Design approval is not prose approval. A preserved legacy workspace is not active authority. Review and audit text never enters the manuscript.
