## Story situation

Morning 14 begins in the family home-and-votive shop where ordinary trade and household life share the same space. Khanh is only temporarily back after several years of Japan-based software work; at home he is again bà Lan's son and available family labor, not a professional observer assigned to a case. Bà Lan has a shop whose money, stock, custom orders, pickups and interruptions continue whether village news is interesting or not.

Cường died suddenly during the night. News of that death is already capable of entering ordinary local conversation, but it does not suspend commerce or turn the shop into an inquiry room. The important pressure is that the shop still has unfinished responsibilities from before the death. Among them is a custom paper-radio order for Cường, recorded while he was alive. When ordinary shop responsibility and fresh death-news occupy the same morning, the order acquires a meaning nobody at the shop asked it to have.

Chợ Bến is already a destination for ordinary buying, selling and errands. Bà Lan/Khanh's route toward it and Lan Anh's funeral-related errands exist independently. Their later public collision exposes the impossible order socially; it is not an arranged verification trip. The chapter eventually returns to Cường's household, where ordinary movement through a crowded death-house is physically deformed by the approved haunting.

## Reader pressure

The reader should feel an ordinary morning becoming harder to place as different kinds of reality remain active at once: fallible death-news, fixed shop obligations created before the death, social embarrassment around ownership and family history, and later direct physical supernatural pressure. These do not need to present themselves as a clean escalation ladder or receive equal dramatic emphasis.

The dread vector is a loss of confidence in ordinary categories rather than an increase in scare frequency. Death-news can remain incomplete village testimony while the shop's finished object, handwritten order record and customer responsibility belong to a harder mundane reality. Their relation may become legible unevenly: a fact can be available before anyone treats it as important, and work can continue without giving each mismatch a dedicated pause.

The radio remains materially ordinary and performs nothing supernatural. Its pressure comes from staying inside real shop life after its social placement has become difficult: it occupies space, belongs to an already-paid custom obligation, has a named recipient who has just died, and can still require somebody to store, move, carry, hand over or bring back. Those practical consequences can keep the wrongness reader-visible without Khanh or the narrator explaining what the reader should feel.

By the movement toward Chợ Bến, ordinary commerce should still be functioning and the trip should still have reasons of its own. The unresolved object can enter that public circulation as one burden among other errands. The reader may understand that several facts sit badly together before any character decides what they mean; Khanh does not need to sustain conscious fear, investigation or a polished interpretation for that pressure to remain active.
## Concrete reality needed

- The family shop is a long-established home-and-shop on a main road beside the Chợ Bến market crossroads in a fictional semi-urbanizing hometown. It sells ordinary stocked votive goods, handles custom orders and does limited finishing/customization; not every item is made in-house.
- Bà Lan is the primary operator. Her ordinary work includes serving customers, handling change, stock, custom-order responsibility and errands while family life interrupts the same space.
- The shop ledger is a blue nylon-covered notebook kept as a practical business record. Bà Lan's entries are handwritten and imperfect rather than a formal database.
- Custom paper objects are commercially plausible. When the radio becomes relevant, it is a handmade old-style cassette-radio model built from thin bamboo strips and paper, with a cloudy cellophane cassette window and a foil-wrapped bamboo antenna. Its existence is not inherently supernatural.
- Khanh, 27, has recently returned from several years of Japan-based software work and is home only for a short break before returning to his Vietnam–Japan technology job in Hanoi.
- Lan Anh is Cường's daughter-in-law and is under immediate household/funeral pressure. Contemporary funeral preparation can begin quickly after a death, but preparation does not imply the body has already been released, encoffined or handled outside professional/legal requirements.
- Chợ Bến is a trading/social network, not an omniscient village chorus. People there have their own business and hear only parts of other people's conversations.

## Non-negotiable locks

- Presentation is morning 14; the full mùng-10 transaction remains CH-02 material.
- Main shop/market focalization is limited third through Khanh. The final household coda is short objective off-POV.
- Shop record: recipient Vũ Văn Cường / xóm Trại; custom paper radio; purchaser Vũ Thị Thoa; order date mùng 10.
- Death news may include a grotesque-looking neck/head detail only as attributed local testimony, not narrator-certified forensics.
- The shop/order/market route follows locked E1 human causality: ordinary shop life, death-news interruption, ordinary work continuing, Cường's order becoming a practical shop responsibility, independent Chợ Bến errands, incidental public collision.
- Lan Anh does not know Thoa's buried 1996 truth. Toàn recognizes the name as dangerous, but the reason remains unrevealed.
- After Lan Anh refuses the radio, it returns to the family shop's custody.
- The coda uses the approved body-sized obstruction near Cường's death site; apparently empty space physically resists/deflects a mundane object strongly enough to change practical movement.
- Do not reveal that Thoa is dead, the supernatural killer, or the objective truth behind the mùng-10 purchaser.

## Open composition space

The Writer owns the local route inside E1: exact shop work, transient customers, what Khanh is doing before he is pulled into work, how long talk lasts, what gets interrupted or abandoned, how bà Lan multitasks, what ordinary errand takes them toward Chợ Bến, scene/summary balance, connective narration, interior association, humor, irritation, silence and incidental problems.

Information does not have to arrive cleanly or receive an immediate response. Shop work may swallow a topic for a while. A person may remember something late, answer only part of what was said, or care more about a practical problem than the reader does.

Hard facts do not need solitary dramatic presentation. Their availability, the moment a character notices them, and the moment the reader connects them may be different. Let practical shop needs and competing attention determine when an order detail is handled rather than constructing a prompt-and-answer path toward it.
