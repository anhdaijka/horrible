# Character context — CH-01-v6

> Writer-safe people context for B10. Present life generates behavior; knowledge remains a boundary rather than a required line.

## POV life at entry — Khanh

Khanh is 27 and only temporarily back in the family home after several years of onsite software work in Japan. His next ordinary life is not this village mystery: it is work, independence and a Vietnam–Japan technology routine centered in Hanoi. Returning home puts him back into an older identity where his mother can treat his time as family time and where the shop, local faces and market traffic are familiar enough not to deserve constant description or analysis.

At home his default is physically loose rather than professionally composed. He can be sleepy, lazy, amused, petty, distracted, nostalgic, embarrassed or uninterested. His analytical/root-cause mode exists, but it is not always switched on. It sharpens when something genuinely refuses ordinary explanation; even then curiosity competes with inconvenience, family boundaries and the possibility that something is none of his business.

Close-third may move associatively with him rather than turning his thoughts into polished deductions. His childhood fear of ghosts remains bodily memory, but adult fear need not announce itself through a fixed set of symptoms. His thoughts may stop at a concrete inconvenience, an unfinished comparison or a provisional ordinary explanation. He does not need to articulate the packet's reader-pressure logic for the reader to feel a mismatch.

## Relationships with history

### Khanh ↔ bà Lan

She is his mother, 55, and the primary operator of the family shop. He has grown up around a home in which shop work and family life overlap, so neither needs to narrate basic routines to the other. Their familiarity permits shorthand, teasing, complaints, unfinished exchanges, being ignored while the other works, and resuming a topic later without formal transition.

Bà Lan is practical, quick and socially observant. Her hands and attention are accustomed to paper, string, adhesive, bags, notes, change and stock. She can keep talking while doing something else. Affection is allowed to arrive through work—feeding him, assigning him something, correcting how he handles an item, assuming he will help—rather than through explicit sentimental dialogue.

Khanh does not need to win small arguments with her. Bà Lan does not need to stop being a shopkeeper in order to be his mother.

### Bà Lan ↔ local customers

She knows people through repeated trade and local social context. She may remember a face, household or unusual request more readily than an exact date. Customers can be referred to through familiar local identifiers rather than introduced for the reader.

Her belief in spiritual possibilities coexists with ordinary skepticism. She can relay something somebody said without certifying it as truth and can end a strange topic simply because another job needs doing.

### Bà Lan ↔ Lan Anh

They have local/trade familiarity, not investigator/witness roles. Lan Anh is Cường's 27-year-old daughter-in-law and on morning 14 is under sudden-death and funeral pressure. Practical politeness, embarrassment and the wish to reduce extra trouble matter more to her than giving a complete account.

### Khanh ↔ Lan Anh / Toàn

Khanh knows them as village acquaintances. Toàn is older. No special intimacy is required in CH-01. Their later household pressure should not be converted into explanatory familiarity for Khanh.

## Kinship / address / register map

- **Khanh ↔ bà Lan:** fixed mother/son relationship; bà Lan is 55 and Khanh is 27. Their home/shop register is intimate and familiar. Exact sentence-level pronouns may vary naturally, but the relationship must not be recast through unrelated kinship or status language.
- **Cường ↔ Toàn:** Cường is Toàn's father; Toàn is Cường's eldest son and is male. When either is identified through the other, preserve that direction and gender. Do not improvise a gendered classifier or family label that changes who Toàn is.
- **Cường ↔ Lan Anh:** Cường is Lan Anh's father-in-law; Lan Anh is his daughter-in-law. No deeper family explanation is required in this chapter.
- **Khanh ↔ Toàn:** village acquaintances; Toàn is older than Khanh. Do not upgrade that into kinship or special intimacy.
- **Bà Lan ↔ Lan Anh:** local/trade familiarity, not kinship.
- **Bà Lan ↔ Cường:** local/customer familiarity is sufficient for CH-01; no family relation is established here.

Where an exact local pronoun, nickname, diminutive, honorific or classifier is not established above, leave it open. Natural Vietnamese wording may be chosen, but it must preserve the known relation, gender/generation/status and must not manufacture local color by inventing a social fact.

## Immediate ordinary concerns and agency

### Khanh
He can help because his mother asks, resist because he is home on a break, look for food/rest, do the easiest useful thing, become mildly embarrassed by local attention, postpone a question, or decide a practical explanation is enough for now. A strange fact may catch him without obligating him to pursue it.

### Bà Lan
She wants the shop and household to function: customers served, money not lost, orders not mixed up, finished work dealt with, errands completed. Her strong customer memory is useful to commerce before it is useful to mystery. An unresolved radio is first an awkward shop responsibility occupying space and ownership, not a case to investigate. If she consults the order notebook, the immediate reason should belong to that responsibility—checking what has been promised, paid, assigned, collected or needs handling—not to a desire to establish mystery evidence.

### Lan Anh
She wants to get through urgent family errands, reduce additional questions and responsibilities, and return to the household. Her refusal of the radio can be abrupt or socially awkward because her bandwidth is already consumed. She does not know the buried truth behind Thoa.

### Toàn
At home he is Cường's eldest son under grief, household duty and severe private pressure. He knows enough family history for the name Thoa to be dangerous to him and can try to contain exposure, but CH-01 must not explain the reason through his interiority or a confession.

### Bystanders
They may gossip, correct, exaggerate, sympathize, buy something, hurry away, remember the wrong part or lose interest. Their lives do not pause to become witness statements.

## Conversational field

Conversation should ride the same practical activity that already holds the scene. Floor ownership can survive short replies, customer service, handling change, packaging, eating, moving stock or a momentary interruption; those events do not automatically reset the exchange.

With Khanh, bà Lan can retain social initiative for a sustained stretch while assigning work, complaining, recalling something, correcting him or talking around a shop task. Khanh need not answer each proposition. He can acknowledge one part, keep working, tease, ignore a branch of what she said, or return to it after another piece of shop business. Their mother-son familiarity supports both compressed shorthand and much longer uneven stretches without requiring symmetrical turns.

With local customers, bà Lan can transact and converse at once. A customer may keep the floor through a story while bà Lan prices, wraps or makes change; bà Lan may interrupt for business and then let the same story continue. Conversely, bà Lan may carry several linked thoughts across practical instructions and gossip while a customer only supplies small acknowledgments. A transaction ending is a stronger floor boundary than every question or gesture inside it.

Khanh's speech at home can be casual and incomplete. He should not sound like the smartest person in every room. His engineer identity becomes more audible only when an actual contradiction earns sustained attention, and even then he is not obliged to turn the exchange into structured questioning.

Lan Anh under pressure may still produce a coherent, socially shaped stretch of speech when she needs to refuse, explain a practical limit or reduce trouble; pressure does not require mechanically fragmented dialogue.

Familiarity does not mean every tease or complaint becomes successful banter. Khanh or bà Lan may ignore a joke because their hands are busy, answer the wrong part, repeat themselves, give an unhelpful acknowledgment, or let a social bid die. A topic may dissolve without a punchline and later resume for a different reason.

No target turn length applies. The governing question is whether social initiative has genuinely transferred, not whether another character has just spoken. No speaker is assigned packet information to verbalize.

## Knowledge / reveal edge

### Khanh
He does not know the 1996 truth, Thoa's fate, Toàn's night-13 scuffle, the clean-nails anomaly or the supernatural killer truth.

### Bà Lan
She personally handled the mùng-10 transaction. CH-02 owns the full experiential replay; CH-01 may use only what current shop responsibility naturally makes present.

### Lan Anh
She knows Cường is dead and the immediate household crisis available to her. She does not know Thoa's actual fate or buried 1996 truth.

### Toàn
He knows the family secret that makes the name Vũ Thị Thoa dangerous to him. He does not know the objective supernatural killer truth.

The household coda is objective/off-POV. No hidden reason becomes narrator knowledge there.
