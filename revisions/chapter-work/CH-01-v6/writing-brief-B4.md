## Core disturbance and reader experience

On the morning after Cường's sudden death, an ordinary family shop discovers that one of its still-open custom orders is for him and was placed while he was alive.

The chapter should feel like village life being forced to accommodate something it cannot comfortably explain—not like people assembling a case.

## Hard story boundary

- Presentation remains on morning 14.
- The mùng-10 transaction belongs to CH-02 and is not replayed in full here.
- The shop's existing order record is: recipient Vũ Văn Cường / xóm Trại; custom paper radio; purchaser Vũ Thị Thoa; order date mùng 10.
- The public Chợ Bến encounter remains the event from which later community gossip can plausibly spread.
- Lan Anh does not know Thoa's buried 1996 truth.
- Toàn's conduct around the name Thoa must remain consistent with his current hidden knowledge, while the reason stays unrevealed.
- After Lan Anh refuses the radio, it returns to the family votive shop's custody.
- The chapter closes in Cường's household with the approved physical haunting: apparently empty human-sized space near the death site can obstruct/deflect a mundane object strongly enough to alter practical movement.
- Do not reveal that Thoa is dead, the supernatural killer, or the objective truth of the mùng-10 purchaser.

These are truth/end-state boundaries, not a required order of evidence delivery.

## Human-causality boundary

- Khanh is not investigating Cường's death. His attention may sharpen when something conflicts with ordinary explanation, but he does not collect a case file.
- Bà Lan is solving shop/household problems, not investigating death, alibi, timeline or family history.
- Lan Anh is handling immediate family pressure and errands, not serving as a mystery witness.
- Bystanders gossip for social reasons, not to provide a witness statement.
- Any consequential question, record lookup, visit, transport of the radio or confrontation needs a sufficient in-world reason at that moment independent of later mystery payoff.

## Dramatic space

The writer owns the concrete route. Information may emerge late, indirectly, partially or without any character recognizing its importance.

The reader may notice a contradiction that characters do not pursue.

Narration may linger, summarize, connect, explain context, follow Khanh's associative thought or let ordinary activity continue beneath conversation.

## Concrete reality

- Bà Lan operates the family votive-paper shop and keeps an order ledger as a practical business record.
- Khanh is temporarily home and may be pulled into shop work.
- Lan Anh is under immediate household/funeral pressure.
- A vivid description of Cường's body/neck may appear only as attributed hearsay/testimony, not narrator-certified forensics.
- Do not imply family preparation overrides required professional handling of the body.

No concrete ritual taboo or universal hàng-mã rule is required.

## Execution freedom

Open: how death news reaches the shop; when/why the Cường order becomes relevant in ordinary shop workflow; what unrelated reason takes bà Lan/Khanh toward Chợ Bến; whether Khanh asks anything at all; dialogue length; local business; transient bystanders; incidental humor/irritation; summary/interiority/connective narration; exact mundane object used in the coda.

Do not choose any of these merely because it is the fastest route to expose a clue.

## Deliberately unanswered

- How accurate the body rumor is.
- Why Toàn treats the name Thoa as dangerous.
- The objective identity/mechanism behind the mùng-10 order.
- The mechanism of the household obstruction.
- What theory Khanh settles on, if any.
