# People at the shop

**Khanh:** 27, software engineer home on a short break after several years working onsite in Japan, still responsible for real Japan-facing work before returning to the Hanoi job. He is practical, sometimes lazy or distracted at home, can believe an ordinary explanation and still remember being frightened by ghosts as a child. He is helping his mother; he is not searching for a case. Narration can call him Khanh/anh, foregrounding bà Lan as mẹ anh when appropriate. He uses con/mẹ; bà Lan can naturally use mày/con/anh with him by situation.

**Bà Lan:** 55, practical, observant, accustomed to working while talking. Customers and household obligations compete with storytelling. She remembers people well but can confuse who first told an account, distinguish something witnessed from hearsay or admit not knowing. She is neither psychic nor a lecturer on supernatural rules. Her name in narration is bà Lan/mẹ Khanh/mẹ anh, never bare Lan. Speak from shared local relations and whatever she is doing, not polished explanatory monologues.

**Other shop customers:** local, chapter-local people; give them concrete purchasing concerns and distinct willingness to talk/listen. Their voices depend on age, familiarity and social purpose. No collective duty to pass evidence to Khanh. Names may be chosen as incidental execution details, with no durable biography inferred.

**The customer from xóm Trại:** a nearby household neighbor, not related to Cường and not an investigator. Arrived after Lan Anh's discovery/call; heard/saw part of the aftermath and comes shopping the next morning. Their knowledge is bounded and their reading of the family's expressions can be wrong. They can remember words imperfectly; the narration must not silently certify guesses.

**Lan Anh in the account:** Cường's daughter-in-law and Toàn's wife; older school acquaintance of Khanh by one or two cohorts. She went on a prearranged visit to her mother, returned and found the hanging, promptly called for help. In this chapter her exhaustion/shock and attempts to attend to people have value on their own. Do not infer romance or case knowledge from earlier acquaintance. The teller did not witness her initial discovery.

**Toàn in the account:** Cường's adult son, a truck driver, Lan Anh's husband. Love and family strain can coexist; the teller sees only outward aftermath. His own private information is not the teller's. He can attempt practical help, fail to hear, ask an ordinary question or keep returning to his father while his family needs him.

**Cụ Cổn in the account:** Cường's 76-year-old father, accustomed to household authority, practical care and imposed decisions. Narrator/village call him cụ Cổn. Avoid turning grief into a proof of guilt or a generic roaring patriarch. Thủy's register if mentioned is thầy/con to her father; u means mother.
