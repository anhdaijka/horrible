# The shop and the conversation

The long-standing home/shop opens onto a busy market crossroads in a fictional semi-urbanizing hometown. Stocked votive goods coexist with occasional finishing/customization. Paper, bags, string, change and items awaiting collection occupy usable space. The family does not make every stocked item itself. A seated customer can watch both a speaker and the entrance; passing vehicles and people occasionally drown out part of a sentence.

Bà Lan knows faces and routines. Purchases have ordinary variations: choosing sizes, discovering an item is elsewhere in the shop, wanting a bag that fits a bike basket, recalling a household list or counting insufficient change. Khanh can help without abandoning his laptop for the entire morning. Laptop work has real continuity: an unfinished task, an unanswered message, a document he has been trying to finish. Do not require a real API/code explanation.

Customers are incidental people with their own household errands, tastes, competing attention and uneven eagerness to tell a story. One may have lived an account; another has only heard it. Bà Lan has her own memory and reservations. The conversation can revisit a detail after an interruption and leave a disagreement unresolved. Nobody needs to frame their speech for an unfamiliar audience who already lives nearby.

Construct the ghost tales from individual household/work/night circumstances. A frightening occurrence should have time, spatial particulars and human responses. Keep each teller's source plain; village hearsay is not a true-history dossier. These tales are fiction inside the fiction, not factual reports of real Vietnamese custom or a source of Case Zero clues.

The internet interruption arises on Khanh's laptop amid work and social browsing. Public posts call a hanging suicide; sparse circulated information precedes the xóm Trại customer's fuller but limited account. The post need not provide a photograph of the body or identifiable house map.

The later customer has an actual purchase/household errand, not a mission to brief the protagonists. Their night was disrupted by a nearby call for help and the gathering afterward; they can recount visible distress, overheard words and their own involvement while trying to finish the current errand. Keep the house description spatially sufficient for the reported experience without locking a new floor plan/room, method, death-time evidence or handling instructions.
