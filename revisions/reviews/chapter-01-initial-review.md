# Chapter 1 Review — `Tiếng chợ`

Status: **original prose superseded; full voice rewrite complete; manuscript review pending**.

## Author feedback that triggered rewrite

The first draft was rejected at the voice level, not merely line-edit level.

Problems identified:

- sentence rhythm was too compressed, with too many short declarative sentences and one-line beats;
- narration felt visibly crafted / linguistically controlled rather than naturally told;
- dialogue sounded rehearsed or television-like instead of lived-in;
- Khanh, Lan, Hùng and Mai risked sharing the same dialogue rhythm;
- supporting characters needed stronger independent agency;
- the prose signaled unease too early, making ordinary family/shop scenes feel pre-haunted;
- this contradicted the intended horror principle: the uncanny should emerge from a world that first feels genuinely familiar, relaxed and everyday.

## Response

A line edit was judged insufficient. The chapter was rewritten from the ground up while preserving the approved structural brief.

Before rewriting, the following canon/style surfaces were recalibrated:

- `author/style-bible.md`
- `characters/nguyen-minh-khanh.md`
- `characters/tran-thi-lan.md`
- `characters/nguyen-van-hung.md`
- `characters/hoang-ngoc-mai.md`

## Rewrite goals

1. Let ordinary life breathe without horror-coded language.
2. Use mixed, more natural sentence lengths and fuller paragraphs.
3. Make dialogue incomplete, contextual and interruption-prone rather than setup/payoff ping-pong.
4. Give Lan, Hùng and Mai practical goals independent of Khanh.
5. Let Khanh be an ordinary son/friend most of the time; reserve his analytical mode for actual anomalies.
6. Keep Khanh–Mai unmistakably childhood-friend / strictly platonic.
7. Make the final upstairs sound a small interruption of a trusted normal pattern rather than the payoff of a chapter already written as ominous.

## Current voice distinctions to protect

### Lan

Shop-first attention; practical, socially observant, able to tell strange stories while also handling customers, prices and household tasks. She does not deliver polished belief statements.

### Hùng

Action-first but not a stock terse father. He speaks from whatever practical task occupies him, may answer sideways, joke dryly, or leave a conversation because he has something else to do.

### Mai

Busy logistics operator with independent work pressure. Her conversation with Khanh is interrupted by drivers and deliveries. Their familiarity comes from shared childhood habits, never romance.

### Khanh

Not permanently in engineer-analysis mode. At home he can be sleepy, petty, amused, embarrassed and childish. Analytical precision should stand out later because it is not the default narrative filter.

## Horror calibration

The chapter should be readable for most of its length as an ordinary homecoming chapter even if the reader did not know the novel was horror.

The strange-story anecdote is normal shop conversation. The final upstairs sound is the first small bodily reminder of Khanh's old fear, and it receives a mundane explanation.

No supernatural claim is established.

## Cultural-reality cleanup

The P0 cultural audit caught one shop-dialogue shorthand (`loại năm mươi / loại tám mươi`) that had no verified referent and risked becoming invented pseudo-ethnography.

It has been removed and replaced with ordinary context-readable retail dialogue. No ritual explanation was invented to preserve it.

The dream/tight-sleeve interaction remains family/shop-specific characterization; it must not be generalized later into a universal Northern ritual rule.
