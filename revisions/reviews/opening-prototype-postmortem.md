# Opening Prototype Postmortem

Status: author-accepted lesson. The old prose itself is rejected and removed from active manuscript.

## What was discarded

- former Chapter 1: `Tiếng chợ`
- former Chapter 2: `Người đặt`
- their active scene records, chapter briefs, chapter-specific reviews and Chapter 1 canon diff

Git history retains them only as development history.

## Why the prototype failed

### 1. Realism became the objective instead of the support system

The opening spent too much narrative time proving that:

- the family was normal;
- the market was normal;
- the shop was normal;
- noises had mundane explanations;
- strange purchases could be normalized.

Those are useful properties, but they became the **content** instead of the foundation beneath a compelling story.

### 2. The novel hid its own genre promise

The old system treated early unease as suspiciously close to cliché and treated mundane resolution as sophistication.

Result: the first meaningful `scare` in Chapter 1 resolved into an ordinary noise, teaching the reader not to expect the book to commit to horror.

### 3. Ambiguity became ideology

The early constitution treated `natural model remains viable` as a preferred outcome rather than one tool among several.

This made the writer repeatedly soften, hedge or explain supernatural possibilities.

New rule: some ghosts are real, some are not, some cases are mixed, some remain unknown.

### 4. Case Zero leaked its own reveal

The Chapter 2 prototype let Khanh explicitly understand that the goods were being ordered **in advance for a living man**.

That forced narrator explanation about serious illness / advance preparation before the story had earned the question.

Correct information order:

1. shop accepts an ordinary order and naturally assumes the goods concern a deceased recipient;
2. later the recipient dies;
3. only then does the order date reveal that he had still been alive when it was placed;
4. only then does `perhaps the family knew he was dying` become a natural hypothesis;
5. later evidence that he had appeared healthy damages that hypothesis.

### 5. Explanation replaced suspense

Repeated explanatory prose told the reader:

- why something was ordinary;
- why Khanh did not care;
- why a cultural practice was plausible;
- why a character was not afraid.

This removed questions instead of creating them.

### 6. `Normal first` was interpreted too literally

The transferable lesson from Vietnamese oral ghost storytelling is not `hide the ghost until enough ordinary pages have passed`.

It is:

> **Give the listener a reason to believe a frightening story is worth hearing, then make its people and practical world believable.**

### 7. Character work was detached from narrative pressure

The prototype improved dialogue, physical description and family agency, but many scenes still existed primarily to demonstrate those improvements.

New opening rule:

- introduce family/shop/market character through the active story;
- do not stop the story to establish baseline unless the baseline creates later contrast.

## What remains valuable

The reset does **not** discard every craft lesson learned.

Keep:

- natural Northern Vietnamese social address;
- distinct character agency;
- physical first-appearance anchors;
- body-language baselines;
- cultural reality research gate;
- dialogue that is interruptible and socially situated;
- shop realism grounded by author domain knowledge;
- delayed meaning;
- social texture;
- ordinary obligations continuing during horror.

But these are tools subordinate to story pull.

## New operating sentence

> **Đừng dùng đời thường để trì hoãn truyện ma. Hãy dùng đời thường để khiến truyện ma có vẻ đã xảy ra ngay cạnh nhà mình.**

## Reuse prohibition

Do not reuse prose, dialogue or scene order from the rejected two chapters by default.

If a later scene independently needs the same mundane fact — e.g. Khanh helps at the shop, Mai passes through the market, bà Lan alters goods — rewrite it fresh in the new narrative context.
