# Prose Depth Audit — Horrible

Status: author-directed craft audit.

## Already strong

The current Style Bible already handles several major failure modes well:

- natural Vietnamese narrative flow rather than visible sentence craft;
- ordinary life before horror;
- differentiated dialogue voice and supporting-character agency;
- Vietnamese social address in narration;
- first-appearance physical anchors;
- body-language baselines and deviation-based horror;
- cultural/religious reality gates;
- oral-story testimony as testimony rather than automatic cosmology;
- ambiguity discipline and mystery kept subordinate to horror.

## Missing or underdeveloped layers

### 1. Focalized attention / free-indirect interiority

The prose should not become either a neutral camera or a stream of explicit `Khanh nghĩ / Khanh nhận ra` explanations. Description can inherit Khanh's priorities, irritation, memory and phrasing without announcing every thought as thought.

Use this selectively. Khanh's attention should reveal character, not become constant commentary.

### 2. Subtext and social permission

Vietnamese family/community scenes gain depth from what a person is socially allowed to ask, joke about, challenge or refuse. A character can answer the surface question while avoiding the real one.

Subtext should come from relationship, age, shame, duty, money, reputation and unfinished history — not from everyone speaking cryptically.

### 3. Show vs tell + scene vs summary control

`Show, don't tell` is not a universal command. Dramatize moments where uncertainty, body language, moral choice or interpersonal pressure matters. Summarize repetitive errands, routine work, travel, repeated questioning and stretches of time when scene treatment would only inflate the novel.

Strategic telling gives scale and lets the truly important scene breathe.

### 4. Negative space / selective omission

A missing name, an avoided pronoun, a subject change, an unanswered question or a family member who only says half of something can carry history. But omission must have a human reason: embarrassment, hierarchy, fatigue, protection, habit, grief, fear of gossip, or simply not wanting to reopen something.

Do not turn every silence into suspense punctuation.

### 5. Aftermath / behavioral residue

A frightening or morally difficult event should sometimes persist through changed routine rather than an immediate explanatory monologue: someone stops taking a route, leaves a light on, avoids touching an object, changes how they answer a customer, sleeps elsewhere, checks a lock twice, or becomes irritated by a formerly harmless sound.

Residue gives horror duration and character consequence.

### 6. Delayed meaning / recontextualized ordinary detail

An ordinary detail can return later with a new meaning: a handwriting habit, a radio knob, a way of folding paper, a phrase used by an older relative, a route through the market, a photograph crop. The first appearance should not announce symbolism.

Re-reading value comes from changed context, not from heavy foreshadowing.

### 7. Tonal counterpoint / simultaneous life

Do not isolate `horror scenes` from the rest of life more than necessary. Grief can coexist with price negotiation, fear with hunger, ritual with parking problems, family resentment with hospitality, and an uncanny clue with somebody needing to answer a work call.

Counterpoint makes the horror feel embedded in life instead of staged for the reader.

### 8. Epistemic texture in prose

The narrator should naturally distinguish what Khanh sees, what somebody says, what he infers and what remains uncertain without turning scenes into evidence tables. Small phrasing differences matter: `ông ấy bảo`, `Khanh nhớ là`, `trông như`, `có vẻ`, `anh không chắc`, versus narrator-certified statements.

This supports the novel's core rule: observed ≠ interpreted ≠ canon.

## Practical priority

Highest-value additions for the next chapters:

1. subtext/social permission;
2. scene-vs-summary control;
3. aftermath/residue;
4. delayed meaning;
5. focalized attention/free-indirect prose.

The other layers should support these rather than become visible technique.

## Anti-overengineering rule

Do not create a checklist score for every paragraph. These are revision lenses. Use only the ones relevant to the scene.

The goal remains: **the reader experiences a richer life, not a writer demonstrating more craft tools.**
