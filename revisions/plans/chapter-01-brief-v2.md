# Chapter 1 Brief v2 — Fresh Opening

Status: **prepared for author review; not yet approved for drafting**.

## Chapter function

Open the novel with a **real ghost-story / uncanny promise** while introducing Khanh, his family shop and Northern Vietnamese social texture through the active Case Zero story.

The chapter must make the reader want to know **how an order for a man could already exist before his unexpected death**.

It must not spend the opening proving that ordinary life is ordinary.

## Working title

Unlocked.

Current candidate only: `Bốn ngày trước`.

Do not treat this as final without author approval.

## POV / narrative frame

Primary POV: close third-person limited around Khanh.

Narrator may use a controlled retrospective frame at the opening:

- show a consequence after the victim's death;
- establish that the victim's name already exists in an earlier shop order;
- then rewind to the original purchase.

The narrator may know that the chronology matters. Khanh in the rewind does not.

## Opening hook

### Preferred beat

A member of the dead man's household comes to bà Lan's shop during early funeral preparation.

During the practical transaction, the victim's name connects naturally to an existing custom-order record / tagged order waiting in the shop.

Bà Lan or Khanh checks the order date.

The date is earlier than the death.

Stop before explaining how or why.

### Reader question

> Tại sao đồ mã cho người đàn ông này đã được đặt từ trước khi ông ấy chết?

This is the Chapter 1 genre promise.

## Rewind

Move back to the original order several days earlier.

Use the rewind to introduce only what the story needs:

- Khanh has recently returned from Japan;
- he is home temporarily before normal Hanoi work resumes;
- his family runs the votive-paper shop;
- bà Lan is competent and busy;
- ông Hùng exists within the household/shop economy but appears only if the scene naturally needs him;
- the market is inhabited, commercial and socially connected.

Do **not** run a separate `meet the family` sequence.

## Purchaser first appearance

The woman should be memorable enough for later recognition but not marked as supernatural.

Before drafting, establish a fresh physical baseline rather than copying the rejected prototype.

Use 2–4 integrated anchors, such as:

- age impression;
- build;
- hands;
- clothing appropriate to weather / locality;
- how she carries her bag / money / paper;
- one ordinary movement later useful for CCTV comparison.

Do not use:

- pallor;
- unnatural cold;
- empty eyes;
- archaic speech;
- spectral clothing;
- a narrator claim that she was `too normal`.

## Original order logic

At this time, the shop naturally understands the order as goods intended for a deceased person.

Nobody asks whether the recipient is alive.

Nobody says:

- `cô đặt trước à?`
- `ông ấy yếu lắm à?`
- `nhà biết không qua khỏi à?`

Nobody explains advance-preparation practice to the reader.

That reasoning belongs only **after** the chronology is discovered later.

## Commercial realism

Before prose, verify the exact scene mechanics against:

- author family trade knowledge;
- `research/verified/votive-paper-commerce-v1.md`;
- `research/verified/votive-shop-language-ordering-v1.md`;
- `research/verified/case-zero-order-cultural-audit-v1.md`.

Safe design principles:

- no universal `funeral package`;
- ordinary male paper goods can remain simple;
- custom old cassette radio may require an outside maker / supplier;
- three-day pickup is customer-requested, not an industry rule;
- recipient identity / address / age fields are shop/admin/purchaser-specific, not metaphysical requirements;
- buyer identity and recipient identity should remain separable in the shop record if this survives fresh review.

## Fair-play details

Possible details to include naturally, subject to fresh confirmation:

- date of order;
- recipient name;
- purchaser name;
- locality / identifying information;
- cassette-radio design;
- three-day pickup request;
- left-hand behavior.

Do not spotlight them with clue language.

The working names from the rejected prototype are unlocked and must **not** be assumed.

## Scene pressure

Even though the order itself appears ordinary to Khanh, the reader already knows from the opening frame that something is wrong with its chronology.

Therefore the rewind does not need artificial ominous wording.

Pressure comes from **dramatic irony**:

- reader knows the named man will die;
- purchaser and shop behave ordinarily;
- every administrative detail becomes potentially memorable without narrator explanation.

## Characterization targets

### Khanh

Show only what the active scene naturally exposes:

- recently returned;
- familiar with the shop but not running it full-time;
- socially at home yet slightly displaced by years away;
- not permanently analytical;
- helps because the shop needs hands, not because he is already an investigator.

### Bà Lan

Show competence through trade behavior.

Her physical/body-language baseline should be refreshed before drafting. Later interruption of that baseline can carry fear when the chronology is discovered.

### Ông Hùng

Optional in Chapter 1 unless a practical job gives him reason to be present.

Do not include him merely to complete family exposition.

### Mai

Not required in Chapter 1.

Introduce later when her own life naturally intersects the case/homecoming. Strictly platonic lock remains.

## Chapter end

Preferred ending after the rewind:

- the custom order is labeled / logged / set aside to wait for pickup;
- life continues around it;
- narrator does not explain the symbolism.

Because the opening has already shown the later death connection, the ordinary stored order should carry dread by itself.

Alternative ending is allowed if it creates equal or stronger forward pressure without leaking future clues.

## What Chapter 1 must NOT reveal

- why the victim dies;
- whether his death is murder / accident / supernatural;
- that he was outwardly healthy at order time;
- the advance-preparation hypothesis;
- purchaser resemblance to a surviving sister;
- purchaser name belonging to a long-dead sibling;
- grandfather connection;
- old family tragedy;
- handedness interpretation;
- final metaphysical model.

## Horror test

Before approval, ask:

1. Would a reader who bought a Vietnamese ghost novel know within the opening pages that the book intends to deliver?
2. Does the chapter create a compelling question without answering it?
3. Is ordinary shop life carrying story pressure rather than replacing it?
4. Does the rewind gain dread from what the reader knows, without characters acting on future knowledge?
5. Is there at least one image/fact likely to remain in memory after the chapter?
