# Vũ Thị Thủy

## Role

The surviving younger sister of the deceased ông Vũ Văn Cường. She is a 51-year-old garment factory worker in Yên Phong, Bắc Ninh. 

Her appearance and identity form the central impossible contradiction in Case Zero:
- The woman in the CCTV footage who ordered funeral goods in advance has Thủy's exact facial features (flat broken nose bridge, prominent cheekbones, slanted eyes, small mole beside left nostril).
- However, the real Thủy possesses an airtight biometric fingerprint alibi at her factory, and has a physically deformed left wrist (a childhood compound fracture at age 12 that healed improperly, leaving a protruding bone knob and clawed, immobile fingers). She cannot hold bowls or chopsticks with her left hand, whereas the entity in the video sketched a cassette radio with fluid, graceful left-handed strokes.
- When bà Lan asks her about the name **Vũ Thị Thoa** over the phone, Thủy reacts with visceral terror, screaming that no one is permitted to speak that name again.

## Core traits

Exhausted, anxious, weathered by decades of factory labor and personal tragedy. She bears the weight of a dark family secret connected to her childhood and the forbidden name "Thoa".

## Physical presence

Around 51 years old, thin, gaunt, with pale, sallow skin from years working in textile factories. 

Key physical anchors:
- Facial features: slightly flattened nose bridge from an old childhood fall, high cheekbones, slightly upturned/slanted eyes, and a hidden dark mole to the left of her nostril.
- The left wrist: thickened and deformed with a prominent bony protrusion the size of a thumb joint on the outer wrist; the four fingers are contracted inward like a boiled chicken claw and cannot be extended flat.
- Clothing: modest, worn working-class garments (purple quilted jacket, dark wool scarf, loose trousers).

## Narrative reference

Referred to in narration as `cô Thủy`. Addressed by bà Lan as `cô Thủy` or `Thủy`.

## Voice

Tremulous, hoarse, carrying deep exhaustion and sudden spikes of panic when forced to confront family history or spiritual anomalies.
