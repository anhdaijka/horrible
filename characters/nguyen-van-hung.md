# Nguyễn Văn Hùng

## Role

Khanh's father. He has a practical construction/technical background and supports the family shop without being its primary operator.

## Core traits

Quiet, action-first and less verbally expansive than Lan. He grew up closer to Khanh's grandfather and therefore carries some family memory that Khanh does not have, but his silence is not a conspiracy device.

Hùng is not emotionally blank and should not read like a stock stoic father. He can be dry, amused, mildly stubborn, distracted by the thing in his hands, or unexpectedly specific when a practical detail matters to him.

## Narrative reference

In Khanh-centered manuscript narration, prefer `bố anh` / `bố Khanh` when the family relation is active. When using his name, write `ông Hùng`, never bare `Hùng`.

## Physical presence

Ông Hùng is 57, medium-tall and lean with the compact strength of someone who has spent years around construction, repair and practical work. His forearms are darker from sun exposure, his knuckles and fingertips visibly rougher than Khanh's, and his short hair is already mixed with gray.

He tends to wear practical layers he can work in and will roll or push sleeves out of the way without thinking. When inspecting a small fault, he brings his face closer and narrows his eyes rather than narrating the problem aloud.

## Body-language baseline

His ordinary baseline is task-anchored: hands occupied, weight settled toward whatever he is fixing, carrying or checking. He often listens without fully turning toward the speaker.

Concern can appear as a tool stopping mid-motion, an unnecessary second check, a longer look before he resumes work, or a decision to put an object down before answering. Genuine fear should be especially noticeable if his normal practical follow-through disappears.

Do not make silence itself ominous; compare it to what his hands and attention normally do.

## Agency

Hùng usually has a task of his own: repairing, checking, carrying, going somewhere, dealing with a household or construction matter, or deciding something does not need further discussion.

He should not remain in a scene merely to supply one terse reaction. He can leave halfway through a conversation because he has somewhere to go, answer from another room, or ignore a question that seems pointless to him.

His avoidance of some grandfather-related matters comes from experience and judgment, not from an authorial need to preserve mystery.

## Voice

He often speaks from whatever he is doing instead of orienting himself fully toward the conversation. His lines may be short, but they should not all be clipped or solemn.

He may answer sideways, correct a practical detail instead of the emotional premise, mutter a dry comment without waiting for a response, or let an unfinished sentence stand because everyone in the family already understands the rest.

Do not use him as a convenient decoder for grandfather's records, and do not make every silence ominous.
