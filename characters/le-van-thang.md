# Lê Văn Thắng

## Role

Khanh's former high school classmate from the district high school (trường huyện). Thắng represents the grounded, ordinary trajectory of rural youth who stayed behind: married early, has a child, and works as a technician at the glass and aluminum workshop near the market crossroads.

In Chapter 3 (`Rạp tang xóm Trại`), Thắng appears at ông Vũ Văn Cường's wake to help with manual chores (carrying chairs, boiling tea, serving visitors) because his maternal cousin-aunt (cô họ bên ngoại) is Cường's sister-in-law.

## Core traits

Warm, extroverted, practical, and unpretentious. Thắng is quick to offer help and comfortable with physical work, but like many young men in the village, he remains awkward and inexperienced with complex funeral rites and death taboos. 

He maintains an easy, humorous camaraderie with Khanh that immediately pierces Khanh's social anxiety upon returning from Japan.

## Narrative reference

In Khanh-centered close-third narration, bare `Thắng` or `thằng Thắng` is natural. Older village elders address him as `thằng Thắng`.

## Physical presence

Thắng is 27, of solid, sturdy build from daily workshop labor. He has a square jaw, thick dark eyebrows, a crew cut (tóc cắt cua), and a wide, easy grin. 

He typically dresses in durable, practical workwear: a grey fleece jacket with sleeves pushed past the elbows, work trousers, and carries a pack of Thăng Long cigarettes in his pocket.

## Body-language baseline

Physically energetic and expressive: clapping a friend on the shoulder, firmly gripping a forearm, wiping sweat with the back of his hand, and gesturing loosely while talking.

When faced with formal ritual matters or reprimands from elders, his default reaction is to scratch his head sheepishly, smile sheepishly, and deflect with self-deprecating humor.

## Relationship with Khanh

Old high school classmates who shared years of cycling together to school. Their dynamic is built on casual peer equality: teasing each other about physical changes (Khanh gaining weight and looking pale after Japan; Thắng married early with a child yet still dorky about village customs). 

Thắng serves as an authentic local anchor for Khanh, providing immediate neighborhood news and offering a mundane social space (drinking at the snail stall by the crossroads) contrasting with the supernatural dread surrounding Khanh's family.

## Voice

Informal Northern rural vernacular: uses `mày — tao` with Khanh, respectful yet cheerful `bác — cháu` with village elders. Speech is direct, colloquial, unpolished, and punctuated with lively exclamations.
