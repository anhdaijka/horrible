# Trần Thị Lan

## Role

Khanh's mother and the primary operator of the family's votive-paper shop. She has strong memory for customers, faces, orders and local social context.

## Core traits

Practical, quick and socially observant. She believes many spiritual experiences are possible but does not treat every story as fact. She is the strongest recurring source of oral ghost stories in the opening world, often preserving the distinction between what somebody told her and what she herself knows.

Lan is not present in scenes to explain the spiritual world to Khanh. She has a shop to run, customers to remember, stock to move, prices to settle and family errands to assign.

## Narrative reference

In Khanh-centered manuscript narration, prefer `mẹ anh` / `mẹ Khanh` when the relationship is foregrounded. When using her name, write `bà Lan`, never bare `Lan`. Internal canon/planning files may still use `Lan` as a precise identifier.

## Physical presence

Bà Lan is 55, slightly small in build but sturdy from years of standing, lifting stock and working a cramped shop counter. Her dark hair shows silver at the temples and is usually twisted or clipped/bundled out of the way while she works.

Her hands are one of the easiest ways to recognize her: dry from paper, string, adhesive and washing; nails kept short; movements quick and economical around bags, notes, change and stock.

## Body-language baseline

Her ordinary baseline is multitasking motion. She can keep a conversation going while counting money, folding paper, reaching for stock or watching the doorway.

Because that motion is normal, unease can register strongly when an automatic sequence breaks: counting the same notes twice, leaving a hand resting on a bag, forgetting the next shop action, looking past a waiting customer, or becoming unusually still before answering.

Do not translate every worry into trembling. For bà Lan, interruption of practiced shop rhythm is often more revealing.

## Agency

Her default priority is whatever the shop or household actually needs at that moment. She may interrupt a story to sell something, forget to finish a point, send Khanh to do a task, change subject because another customer has arrived, or decide a question is not worth answering.

Her memory is socially strong but not infallible. She can remember a face better than a date, confuse who first told a story, or retell the same event with small changes years later.

## Voice

Lan speaks from context rather than in polished statements. She uses shared family/local knowledge without explaining it, refers to customers through relationships or familiar identifiers, and often folds gossip, practical judgment and strange stories into the same conversation.

Her uncertainty should usually sound ordinary rather than philosophical: `thì người ta kể thế`, `mẹ cũng có biết đâu`, `hình như`, `hay là...`, `thôi kệ`, or simply moving on to the next job.

She can be funny without trying to deliver a punchline. She can be impatient, distracted, affectionate through work, and occasionally sharper than Khanh expects.

She does not function as a psychic or supernatural authority.

## Case Zero shop pressure — D-002

Bà Lan’s record of the mùng 10 custom order becomes a practical family responsibility amid rumors and customer pressure. The name, design and order date must not drift to suit outsiders’ accounts. On morning mùng 10 the radio exists as a request/sketch; the finished paper object can be present by morning 14. There is no automatic duplicate payment when Lan Anh comes to prepare funeral goods.

Under real shop pressure, her questions may be strictly operational: `Cường nhà nào`, what model/detail is wanted and when it will be collected. This is competent disambiguation, not prying into why the customer wants the item. She does not need to ask whether the household knows, demand authorization or warn Cường. If she identifies the exact household on mùng 10, however, morning-14 prose must preserve that memory rather than stage Cường's identity as a new discovery.

## D-015 — Author-approved shop ignorance and realistic mùng10/mùng14 crossover

Bà Lan has known Lan Anh since she went to the same **district high school** as Khanh (Lan Anh 1–2 cohorts older), liked him deeply in school and later unexpectedly married Toàn from distant **xóm Trại**, whose elderly family patriarch **Cổn** is locally known for authoritarian behavior. But bà Lan **does NOT know Lan Anh's father-in-law is Vũ Văn Cường**, and does not even know her husband's family surname is Vũ. Distant xóm Trại is NOT an adjoining shop lane or a village where Lan knows everyone's lineage; three different people are called Cường there: deceased Vũ Văn Cường, a grade-7 pupil, a fit 78-year-old living in a lane farther up. A customer self-named Thoa on **mùng10** fully prepaid an unusual-but-ordinary custom red Czech paper radio addressed to **Vũ Văn Cường**; in busy rằm-season shop work bà Lan has no reason to know whether that recipient is alive or deceased or whether this is funeral/anniversary/general worship. Her questions if any concern correct design, money, deadline and **already recorded/arranged handoff**. Hearing vague news about “ông Cường xóm Trại” died on mùng13 is not proof it is the same person. **Only S03** Lan Anh's normal funeral errand at Chợ Bến plus sufficient original name/handoff/address/contact verification link that named recipient to her just-dead father-in-law, whereupon Lan Anh publicly refuses the item and it returns to the shop. **S04** rereads the mùng10 customer memory AFTER that earned discovery; the late question is what the customer knew and why that radio. Long later arrives from PUBLIC market gossip, never a private Lan Anh tip. Bà Lan did not knowingly take a ghostly order for a man she knew was living. See [D-015](../author/decisions.md).

## D-016 — Author-approved dialogue direction

Bà Lan's working-shop voice is practical and relationship-specific, never a universal one-liner machine. To adult son Khanh she MAY naturally say **mày**, sometimes **con** or situational reproachful **anh**, with warmth or impatience appropriate to real work; the choice isn't a quota. She can demand useful help while recognizing that her 27-year-old son has genuine Japan-facing remote obligations and may refuse a full delivery run. To a much younger customer she adjusts **cô/cháu, chị/em** to real age/relationship and explains actual deadlines and costs without using the client only as a setup for a joke. Unlike Cổn's power-laden mày, her familiar mày doesn't make obedience a lifetime debt. She may stop a conversation to complete paid work; do not give all shop exchanges a complaint→short reply→punchline structure. See [Voice Bible](../plot/case-zero-voice-and-dialogue-bible-v1.md). The old A-RAW is preferred for lived-in texture, NOT approved final dialogue.
