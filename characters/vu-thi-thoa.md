# Vũ Thị Thoa

## Role

The forgotten second child / middle daughter of the Vũ family in xóm Trại, who died tragically in the summer of 1996 at age 22 (born 1974). She was the younger sister of Vũ Văn Cường and the older sister of Vũ Thị Thủy.

Under master canon (**D-056** & **DIR-02**), Thoa's death and memory constitute the dark supernatural heart of Case Zero:
- **Forced marriage dispute & fatal manslaughter (1996):** In summer 1996, the Vũ family urgently needed wedding funds for eldest son Cường (to marry the mother of Toàn). A wealthy local family's son was madly infatuated with Thoa's delicate beauty and offered a lavish bride price (refusing Thủy due to her clubbed left hand). When Thoa fiercely refused to be sold off as a sacrificial pawn for Cường's wedding, an explosive quarrel erupted by the lotus pond. Cường struck and shoved Thoa, causing her to slip and smash the back of her head against a submerged boulder on the pond bank, knocking her senseless.
- **The "faking" misjudgment & late discovery:** A cursory check and a missed opportunity to summon help allow Cường and Cổn to cling to the self-serving “faking” interpretation and abandon her. Thủy finds her later. Exact checking actions require preflight; neither witness perception nor narration supplies an unsupported medical clock or certainty about salvageability.
- **Patriarchal concealment:** Cổn orders concealment, Cường hides the body, Thủy is coerced into silence. The genealogy erasure and “fled with a lover” story suppress her identity. No 1996 recording exists in the evidence plot. The radio carries personal memory; witness knowledge, remains and independent corroboration carry the historical investigation under D-002.
- **Disturbance and return (2026):** Work at the pond before mùng 10 disturbs an already weakened buried structure. There is no exact thirty-year expiration. Cường again depriving Thủy supplies the moral repetition. The advance order reclaims Thoa’s erased name while announcing Cường’s death; the radio recalls her personal life.
- **Supernatural execution:** Thoa inflicts fatal neck injury before Cường impacts the stairs. No fixed angle. Her death is withheld until G6a and her executioner/order identity until G6c; early domestic haunting after Cường dies centers on him.

## Core traits

In life: Quiet, introverted, artistic, gentle, and stubborn in preserving her left-handedness despite severe family discipline.
In death: A frightening, wronged presence whose vengeance does not make her infallible justice. After revenge she still needs acknowledgment of the person her family erased.

## Physical presence (In memory & footage)

In 1996, at age 22 (born 1974):
- Slender, pale skin, delicate bone structure.
- Shared the prominent family bone structure with her siblings (high cheekbones, slanted almond eyes, delicate jawline).
- Her left hand was notably long-fingered, pale, and remarkably agile when sketching or handling objects.
- In the mùng 10 CCTV footage, shared Vũ-family features allow mistaken resemblance to Thủy; this is not exact duplication of Thủy’s face. Fluid left-hand use challenges that identification. No lookalike-thief substitute is active.

## Information-order firewall

Objective author truth is not reader-facing truth. Before G6a in the current phase matrix under **D-001 post-reset / DIR-01**, narration and characters must not identify Thoa as dead, a ghost, or the source of the early domestic haunting. In the investigation layer she remains an enigmatic potentially living missing woman connected to the CCTV identity mystery.

## Narrative reference

Referred to in dialogue and historical recollection as `cô Thoa` or `Thoa` when that information is legitimately available to the speaker.

## Thematic resonance

Represents the silenced, erased victims of patriarchal rural authority and sibling greed, whose suppressed blood-guilt cannot be permanently paved over by concrete or time.

## Personhood and limits — D-002

Preserve ordinary warmth, small flaws and an unfinished personal wish in memories of Thoa; do not reduce her to a beautiful murdered girl. Specific memory anecdotes remain scene choices, not automatically all brainstorm examples.

She knew the local family shop. A custom order and its written name let her leave a trace of acknowledgment; this does not make Khanh’s grandfather a planner of the crime or her return. The radio has a recognizable personal repair/drawn detail, not an encoded confession. She cannot rewrite records, memories or the living characters’ decisions. Her identity may be guessed early; narrator confirmation still respects G6a–G6c.
